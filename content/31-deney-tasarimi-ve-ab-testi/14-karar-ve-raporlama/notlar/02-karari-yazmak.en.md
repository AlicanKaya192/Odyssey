Even when a report's numbers are right, its sentences can be misread.
Common mistakes and what to write instead:

| Not written | Written |
|---|---|
| "B has no effect." | "We could not measure B's effect with this test; the interval is −x to +y." |
| "B raised sales by 15%." | "B raised buying from 11.99 to 13.76 (+1.77 points, 15% relative)." |
| "p = 0.04, so we are 96% sure." | "The difference is most likely not chance (p = 0.04); the interval is ..." |
| "It did not work on mobile." | "Mobile's interval is wide; it can be looked at in a separate test." |
| "3.5 million TL gain a year." | "Extra yearly sales between 2 and 5 million TL (short test, novelty risk)." |

## Who decides?

If the rules were written before the test, the decision mostly follows by
itself. The report **explains** the decision; changing the threshold, the
metric or the duration after the test ends is the same trap as in the
Peeking and Multiple Comparisons sections.

## Owning an inconclusive result

An "inconclusive" result is not a failure: it shows the change did not
create a large effect and sets the size of the next test. The report says
"we cannot rule out an effect as large as the top of the interval; we rule
out anything larger".

## Records

Every test's plan, report and decision are kept in one place. When the
same idea is proposed again two years later, the first question is "have
we tried this?".
