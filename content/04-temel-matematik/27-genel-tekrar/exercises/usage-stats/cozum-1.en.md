**What is asked?** The mean and standard deviation of a dataset and the z-score of one value.

**Idea:** The standard deviation is the square root of the mean of the squared deviations; the z-score is the deviation over the standard deviation.

**Step 1 — The mean.** $\frac{100}{5} = 20$.

**Step 2 — The standard deviation.** The squares are $64, 25, 4, 0, 225$; total $318$; variance $63.6$.

$$
\sigma = \sqrt{63.6} \approx 7.975
$$

**Step 3 — The z-score.** $\frac{35 - 20}{7.975} \approx 1.881$.

**Check:** The deviations add up to $-8 - 5 - 2 + 0 + 15 = 0$ ✓.

**Watch out:** In this data a single large value ($35$) pulls up both the mean ($20$, median $18$) and the standard deviation; that is why the z-score is smaller than you might expect.

**Answer:** $20$, $7.975$, $1.881$.
