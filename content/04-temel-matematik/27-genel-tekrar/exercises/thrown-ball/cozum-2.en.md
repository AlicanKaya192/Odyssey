**Idea:** Bring $h(t)$ into vertex form; all three questions can be read from it.

**Step 1 — Vertex form.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
h(t) &= -5(t^2 - 4t) + 25 \\
&= -5(t - 2)^2 + 20 + 25 = -5(t - 2)^2 + 45
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $(t - 2)^2 \geq 0$, the maximum is $45$, at $t = 2$.

**Step 2 — Hitting the ground.** $-5(t - 2)^2 + 45 = 0$, $(t - 2)^2 = 9$, $t - 2 = \pm 3$: $t = 5$ (or $-1$).

**Step 3 — $40$ m.** $(t - 2)^2 = 1$, $t = 2 \pm 1$; on the way down $t = 3$.

**Why the same result?** The vertex form is the algebra behind the $-\frac{b}{2a}$ formula and the factoring; the symmetry shows plainly here too: every height repeats at two moments equally far on either side of the vertex.

**Answer:** $45$, $5$ and $3$.
