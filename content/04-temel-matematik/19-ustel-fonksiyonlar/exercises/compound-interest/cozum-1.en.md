**What is asked?** The amount after three years of compound interest, the total growth, and the amount when interest is applied more often.

**Idea:** An amount growing at the same rate each period is $A_0 (1 + r)^t$, where $t$ is the number of periods.

**Step 1 — Three years.**

$$
10{,}000 \cdot 1.2^3 = 10{,}000 \cdot 1.728 = 17{,}280
$$

**Step 2 — Total growth.** $\frac{17{,}280}{10{,}000} = 1.728$; a $72.8$ percent increase. Not $60$ percent: each year's interest earns interest the next year.

**Step 3 — Every six months.** Three years hold $6$ periods, each with factor $1.1$:

$$
10{,}000 \cdot 1.1^6 = 10{,}000 \cdot 1.771561 = 17{,}715.61
$$

**Check:** Applying interest more often must give more money: $17{,}715.61 > 17{,}280$ ✓. The yearly comparison says the same: $1.1^2 = 1.21 > 1.2$.

**Watch out:** Computing three years of $20$ percent as $10{,}000 \cdot 1.6$ is simple interest; with compound interest the factors multiply.

**Answer:** $17{,}280$, $72.8$ percent, $17{,}715.61$.
