**Idea:** Instead of the formula, grow the amount period by period; each row multiplies the previous amount by the factor.

**Step 1 — $20$ percent yearly.**

| Year | Interest | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | | $10{,}000$ |
| 1 | $2000$ | $12{,}000$ |
| 2 | $2400$ | $14{,}400$ |
| 3 | $2880$ | $17{,}280$ |

The interest grows every year, because it is computed on a growing amount. The total increase is $7280$, that is $72.8$ percent.

**Step 2 — $10$ percent every six months.** Multiply by $1.1$ every six months: $11{,}000$, $12{,}100$ (end of year 1), $13{,}310$, $14{,}641$ (end of year 2), $16{,}105.1$, $17{,}715.61$ (end of year 3).

**Why the same result?** Each row of the table is one multiplication by $1.2$ (or $1.1$); three rows are $1.2^3$, six rows $1.1^6$. The formula is the table in short.

**Answer:** $17{,}280$, $72.8$ percent and $17{,}715.61$.
