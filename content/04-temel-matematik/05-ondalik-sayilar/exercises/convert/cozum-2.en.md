**Idea:** A fraction whose denominator contains only $2$s and $5$s can be expanded to a power of $10$; then the decimal can be read off at once. In the other direction we factorise the denominator $100$ and simplify.

**Step 1 — Complete $8$ to $1\,000$.** $8 = 2^3$; $1\,000 = 2^3 \cdot 5^3$. What is missing is $5^3 = 125$:

$$
\frac{7}{8} = \frac{7 \cdot 125}{8 \cdot 125} = \frac{875}{1\,000} = 0.875
$$

**Step 2 — Think of $\dfrac{36}{100}$ the other way.** $100 = 4 \cdot 25$ and $36 = 4 \cdot 9$: the common factor is $4$, leaving $\dfrac{9}{25}$.

**Why the same result?** Long division is just multiplying $7$ by powers of $10$ and dividing by $8$; this route did the same product ($7 \cdot 125$) in one go. Because $8$ contains only $2$s, it could be completed to an exact power of $10$; with a fraction like $\frac{1}{3}$ this would not be possible.

**Answer:** $0.875$ and $\dfrac{9}{25}$.
