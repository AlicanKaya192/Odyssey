**Idea:** If we know that $\frac{1}{9} = 0.\overline{1}$ and $\frac{1}{99} = 0.\overline{01}$, we can see repeating decimals as multiples of these.

**Step 1 — $\frac{1}{9}$.** $1 \div 9$: the remainder is $1$ at every step and the quotient digit is always $1$: $\frac{1}{9} = 0.111\dots$

**Step 2 — $0.\overline{4}$.** Every digit is $4$ times as large:

$$
0.444\dots = 4 \cdot 0.111\dots = 4 \cdot \frac{1}{9} = \frac{4}{9}
$$

**Step 3 — $\frac{1}{99}$.** $1 \div 99$: $100 = 99 \cdot 1 + 1$; a $01$ repeats every two places: $\frac{1}{99} = 0.010101\dots$

**Step 4 — $0.\overline{12}$.** $12$ times as large:

$$
0.121212\dots = 12 \cdot 0.0101\dots = \frac{12}{99} = \frac{4}{33}
$$

**Why the same result?** The denominators $9$ and $99$ in the first method come from here: $10 - 1 = 9$ and $100 - 1 = 99$. If the repeating part has $k$ digits, the denominator is $k$ nines; that rule is where the two methods meet.

**Answer:** $\dfrac{4}{9}$ and $\dfrac{4}{33}$.
