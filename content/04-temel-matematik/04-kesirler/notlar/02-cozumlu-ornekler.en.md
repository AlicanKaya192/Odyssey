A worked example for each method in the lesson. Try the question yourself first, then read the solution.

## 1. Simplifying

**Question:** Bring $\dfrac{90}{144}$ to its simplest form.

$90 = 2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5$, $144 = 2^4 \cdot 3^2$; the GCD is $2 \cdot 3^2 = 18$:

$$
\frac{90}{144} = \frac{90 \div 18}{144 \div 18} = \frac{5}{8}
$$

## 2. Comparing

**Question:** Which is larger, $\dfrac{7}{12}$ or $\dfrac{5}{9}$?

Cross-multiply: $7 \cdot 9 = 63$, $5 \cdot 12 = 60$. $63 > 60$, so
$\dfrac{7}{12} > \dfrac{5}{9}$.

## 3. Adding with different denominators

**Question:** What is $\dfrac{3}{8} + \dfrac{5}{6}$?

$\text{LCM}(8, 6) = 24$:

$$
\frac{3}{8} + \frac{5}{6} = \frac{9}{24} + \frac{20}{24} = \frac{29}{24} = 1\tfrac{5}{24}
$$

## 4. Subtracting mixed numbers

**Question:** What is $4\tfrac{1}{4} - 1\tfrac{2}{3}$?

Convert to improper fractions: $\dfrac{17}{4} - \dfrac{5}{3}$. Common
denominator $12$:

$$
\frac{51}{12} - \frac{20}{12} = \frac{31}{12} = 2\tfrac{7}{12}
$$

## 5. Cancelling, then multiplying

**Question:** What is $\dfrac{14}{15} \cdot \dfrac{25}{28}$?

$14$ and $28$ cancel (divide by $14$), and $15$ and $25$ cancel (divide
by $5$):

$$
\frac{14}{15} \cdot \frac{25}{28} = \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{5}{2} = \frac{5}{6}
$$

## 6. Division

**Question:** What is $2\tfrac{1}{2} \div \dfrac{5}{8}$?

$$
\frac{5}{2} \div \frac{5}{8} = \frac{5}{2} \cdot \frac{8}{5} = \frac{8}{2} = 4
$$

Meaning: there are $4$ lots of $\tfrac{5}{8}$ in $2\tfrac{1}{2}$.

## 7. A "fraction of" problem

**Question:** $\tfrac{3}{8}$ of a $240$-page book has been read. How many
pages are left?

Read: $\tfrac{3}{8} \cdot 240 = 90$. Left: $240 - 90 = 150$. Or directly:
the fraction left is $\tfrac{5}{8}$, and $\tfrac{5}{8} \cdot 240 = 150$.

## 8. Order of operations

**Question:** What is $\dfrac{2}{3} - \dfrac{1}{2} \div \dfrac{3}{4}$?

Division first: $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{4}{3} = \dfrac{2}{3}$. Then:

$$
\frac{2}{3} - \frac{2}{3} = 0
$$
