**What is asked?** Head counts from "fraction of" statements, and a ratio at the end.

**Idea:** "$\frac{a}{b}$ of a number" means multiplying the number by $\frac{a}{b}$. We find the size of each group in turn and write the ratio as a fraction at the end.

**Step 1 — Girls and boys.**

$$
\frac{3}{5} \cdot 30 = 18 \text{ girls}, \qquad 30 - 18 = 12 \text{ boys}
$$

**Step 2 — Girls with glasses.**

$$
\frac{1}{3} \cdot 18 = 6
$$

**Step 3 — Boys with glasses.**

$$
\frac{1}{4} \cdot 12 = 3
$$

**Step 4 — The ratio.** $6 + 3 = 9$ with glasses in total:

$$
\frac{9}{30} = \frac{3}{10}
$$

**Watch out:** Writing $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{7}{12}$ and taking that as the class fraction is wrong; those two fractions are parts of different wholes (the girls and the boys) and cannot simply be added.

**Answer:** $6$ girls; $\dfrac{3}{10}$ of the class.
