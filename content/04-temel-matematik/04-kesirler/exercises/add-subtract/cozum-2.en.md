**Idea:** Instead of bringing all three fractions to a common denominator at once, work left to right two at a time. Each step needs only the LCM of two denominators.

**Step 1 — The first two.** $\text{LCM}(3, 4) = 12$:

$$
\frac{2}{3} + \frac{3}{4} = \frac{8}{12} + \frac{9}{12} = \frac{17}{12}
$$

**Step 2 — Subtract the third.** $\text{LCM}(12, 6) = 12$, since $6$ already divides $12$:

$$
\frac{17}{12} - \frac{5}{6} = \frac{17}{12} - \frac{10}{12} = \frac{7}{12}
$$

**Why the same result?** Addition and subtraction go left to right; keeping the intermediate result as a fraction and carrying on is the same as bringing everything to a common denominator at the start. Here the denominator from the first step ($12$) already suited the last fraction, so the second step was easy.

**Answer:** $\dfrac{7}{12}$.
