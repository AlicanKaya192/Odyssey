**What is asked?** An expression with division and multiplication of fractions together.

**Idea:** Turn the division into multiplication by the reciprocal of the divisor. In products, cancel first and then multiply; the numbers stay small.

**Step 1 — Inside the brackets: turn division into multiplication.**

$$
\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{9}{8} = \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{8}{9}
$$

**Step 2 — Cancel.** $3$ with $9$ (divide by $3$: $1$ and $3$), $8$ with $4$ (divide by $4$: $2$ and $1$):

$$
\frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{8}{9} = \frac{1}{1} \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \frac{2}{3}
$$

**Step 3 — The multiplication outside.**

$$
\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{2}{5} = \frac{4}{15}
$$

**Step 4 — Does it simplify?** $4 = 2^2$, $15 = 3 \cdot 5$: no common factor.

**Watch out:** Flipping the first fraction ($\frac{4}{3} \cdot \frac{9}{8}$) is a common slip. It is the divisor, the second fraction, that gets flipped.

**Answer:** $\dfrac{4}{15}$.
