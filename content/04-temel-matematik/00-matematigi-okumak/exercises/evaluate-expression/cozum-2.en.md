**Idea:** Split the expression into terms, work out each term separately, then add them with their signs. This is the tidiest way to avoid mistakes in long expressions.

**Step 1 — Write the terms with their signs.** $2x^2$, $-3x$, $+4$.

**Step 2 — Work out each term for $x = -3$.**

| Term | Substitute | Value |
|---|---|---|
| $2x^2$ | $2 \cdot (-3)^2 = 2 \cdot 9$ | $18$ |
| $-3x$ | $-3 \cdot (-3)$ | $9$ |
| $4$ | $4$ | $4$ |

**Step 3 — Add.**

$$
18 + 9 + 4 = 31
$$

**Why the same result?** In the first method we treated the $-3x$ term as "minus, 3 times $x$"; here we included its coefficient ($-3$) in the term. Both are the same: $-3 \cdot (-3) = +9$. Taking the coefficient with its sign removes the "is it subtraction or addition?" confusion.

**Answer:** $31$.
