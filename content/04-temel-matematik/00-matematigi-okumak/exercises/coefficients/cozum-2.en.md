**Idea:** Sorting the terms by exponent from largest to smallest makes the expression easier to read; expressions in mathematics are usually written this way. When moving terms, **carry their signs with them**.

**Step 1 — Separate the terms with their signs.** $+5x^3$, $-x^2$, $+7$, $-2x$.

**Step 2 — Sort by exponent.** $x^3$ (exponent 3), $x^2$ (exponent 2), $x$ (exponent 1), constant (exponent 0):

$$
5x^3 - x^2 - 2x + 7
$$

**Step 3 — Read the coefficients in order.** $5$, $-1$, $-2$, $7$. Those asked for: the coefficient of $x$ is $-2$, of $x^2$ is $-1$, the constant term is $7$.

**Why does it work?** The order of the terms does not change the value (order does not matter in addition), but a tidy order makes it easy to check that each power appears exactly once. If a power is missing, its coefficient is $0$; here, for example, the coefficient of $x^4$ is $0$.

**Check:** Putting in $x = 1$, both forms must give the same number: $5 - 1 + 7 - 2 = 9$ and $5 - 1 - 2 + 7 = 9$ ✓.

**Answer:** $-2$, $-1$, $7$.
