**What is asked?** Reading a chain inequality and deciding whether each end point is included.

**Idea:** $-4 < x \le 2$ is two conditions at once: "$x$ is greater than $-4$" **and** "$x$ is less than or equal to $2$". A strict inequality ($<$) leaves the end out, $\le$ takes it in.

**Step 1 — Decide on the ends.**

- $-4 < x$: $-4$ is **not** included; the first integer greater than $-4$ is $-3$.
- $x \le 2$: $2$ **is** included.

**Step 2 — List the numbers.**

$$
-3,\ -2,\ -1,\ 0,\ 1,\ 2
$$

Six integers.

**Step 3 — Add.** Pairs of opposites give zero: $(-2) + 2 = 0$, $(-1) + 1 = 0$. What remains is $-3$ and $0$:

$$
-3 + 0 = -3
$$

**Watch out:** Counting before deciding on the ends is the most common slip: counting $-4$ too gives $7$ numbers and a sum of $-7$.

**Answer:** $6$ integers; their sum is $-3$.
