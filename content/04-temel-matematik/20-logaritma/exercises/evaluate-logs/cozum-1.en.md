**What is asked?** The sum of two logarithms. We find each one separately and add them.

**Idea:** A logarithm asks for an exponent: $\log_b x = y$ means $b^y = x$, that is, "to which power must I raise $b$ to get $x$?". We answer this question for each term.

**Step 1 — First term: $\log_3 81$.** Question: which power of 3 is 81? Write the powers of 3 in order:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3^1 &= 3 \\
3^2 &= 9 \\
3^3 &= 27 \\
3^4 &= 81
\end{aligned}
$$

We reach 81 at the 4th power, so $\log_3 81 = 4$.

**Step 2 — Second term: $\log_2 \frac{1}{8}$.** Question: which power of 2 is $\frac{1}{8}$? We know $2^3 = 8$. A negative exponent means "one over": $2^{-3} = \frac{1}{2^3}$.

$$
2^{-3} = \frac{1}{2^3} = \frac{1}{8}
$$

So $\log_2 \frac{1}{8} = -3$.

**Step 3 — Add.**

$$
4 + (-3) = 1
$$

**Watch out:** The logarithm of a number smaller than $1$ (with a base greater than 1) is **negative**. Writing $3$ for $\log_2 \frac{1}{8}$ is the most common slip.

**Answer:** 1.
