**Idea:** One term can be turned into a number right away: the logarithm of the base itself is always 1, because $6^1 = 6$. Putting a known value in early leaves less work.

**Step 1 — Turn the known term into a number.** $\log_6 6 = 1$:

$$
\log_6 12 + \log_6 18 - \log_6 6 = \log_6 12 + \log_6 18 - 1
$$

**Step 2 — Combine the remaining two terms.** With the product rule ($\log_b x + \log_b y = \log_b xy$):

$$
\log_6 12 + \log_6 18 = \log_6 (12 \cdot 18) = \log_6 216
$$

**Step 3 — Write $216$ as a power of 6.** $216 = 6 \cdot 36 = 6 \cdot 6 \cdot 6 = 6^3$. So:

$$
\log_6 216 = \log_6 6^3 = 3
$$

**Step 4 — Do the subtraction.**

$$
3 - 1 = 2
$$

**Why the same result?** The first method brought everything down to a single logarithm and calculated at the end ($\log_6 36$). This one turned a known value into a number at the start. Both use the same rules, only in a different order.

**Answer:** 2.
