**What is asked?** Whether statements built with connectives are true or false.

**Idea:** First find the values of the simple statements, then read the relevant row of each connective's table.

**Step 1 — The simple statements.** $3 > 2$ is true: $p = 1$. $2 + 2 = 4 \neq 5$: $q = 0$.

**Step 2 — "And".** $p \wedge q$: not both are true, $0$.

**Step 3 — "Or".** $p \vee q$: at least one ($p$) is true, $1$.

**Step 4 — $p \Rightarrow q$.** Premise true, conclusion false: the only false row of the table, $0$.

**Step 5 — $q \Rightarrow p$.** The premise is false: the "if–then" statement is true, $1$.

**Reading the result:** That $p \Rightarrow q$ and $q \Rightarrow p$ come out differently shows that the converse of a statement is a separate statement.

**Watch out:** Saying of $q \Rightarrow p$ "the premise is false, so the whole is false" is a common slip; anything follows from a false premise.

**Answer:** $0$, $1$, $0$, $1$.
