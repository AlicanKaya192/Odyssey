**Idea:** Work only with the numbers of elements, without writing the sets. Once the intersection is known, everything else follows from the formulas.

**Step 1 — What we know.** $n(A) = 6$, $n(B) = 5$, $n(A \cap B) = 3$.

**Step 2 — The union.**

$$
n(A \cup B) = 6 + 5 - 3 = 8
$$

**Step 3 — The difference.**

$$
n(A \setminus B) = n(A) - n(A \cap B) = 6 - 3 = 3
$$

**Why the same result?** In a Venn diagram $A$ is made of two regions: $A$ only ($A \setminus B$) and the intersection. The formulas add and subtract these regions; the first route wrote out their elements one by one. With large sets only this route is practical.

**Answer:** $8$, $3$ and $3$.
