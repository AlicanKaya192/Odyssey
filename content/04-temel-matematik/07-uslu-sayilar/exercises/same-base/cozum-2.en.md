**Idea:** The rules come from the definition; if we write the powers out as factors and cancel common ones, we reach the same result without any rule.

**Step 1 — How many $2$s are on top?** $3$ in $2^3$ and $5$ in $2^5$: $8$ twos multiplied in total.

**Step 2 — How many $2$s are on the bottom?** $6$.

**Step 3 — Cancel.** $6$ twos go from top and bottom; $8 - 6 = 2$ remain on top:

$$
\frac{\overbrace{2 \cdot 2 \cdots 2}^{8}}{\underbrace{2 \cdot 2 \cdots 2}_{6}} = 2 \cdot 2 = 4
$$

**Why the same result?** The rule "add the exponents" is a shortcut for counting factors; "subtract the exponents" is a shortcut for counting the ones that cancel. If you forget a rule, this counting always works.

**Answer:** $4$.
