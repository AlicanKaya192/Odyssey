**Idea:** Try small whole numbers. In equations like $2^x = 32$ a whole-number answer appears at once; in $9^y = 27$ we see that the answer lies between two whole numbers, and then we pin it down.

**Step 1 — Try $2^x$.** $2^1 = 2$, $2^2 = 4$, $2^3 = 8$, $2^4 = 16$, $2^5 = 32$ ✓. $x = 5$.

**Step 2 — Try $9^y$.** $9^1 = 9 < 27 < 81 = 9^2$: the answer is between $1$ and $2$, not a whole number.

**Step 3 — Write $27$ in terms of $9$.** $27 = 9 \cdot 3$, and $3$ is the "half power" of $9$ ($3 \cdot 3 = 9$). So $27 = 9^1 \cdot 9^{1/2} = 9^{1 + 1/2}$.

**Step 4 — Read it off.** $y = 1 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2}$.

**Why the same result?** Saying $3 = 9^{1/2}$ is saying $(3^2)^{1/2} = 3^1$: a spoken version of the equality $3^{2y} = 3^3$ from the first method. Because trying values showed the answer lies between $1$ and $2$, it also told us to expect a fractional exponent.

**Answer:** $5$ and $\dfrac{3}{2}$.
