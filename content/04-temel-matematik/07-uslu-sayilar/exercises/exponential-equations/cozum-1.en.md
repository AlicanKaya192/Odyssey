**What is asked?** Two equations with the unknown in the exponent.

**Idea:** If $a^p = a^q$ (same base, $a > 0$, $a \neq 1$), then $p = q$. Writing both sides as powers of the same base lets us equate the exponents and get an ordinary equation.

**Step 1 — $2^x = 32$.** $32 = 2^5$:

$$
2^x = 2^5 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 5
$$

**Step 2 — $9^y = 27$: find a common base.** $9$ and $27$ are both powers of $3$: $9 = 3^2$, $27 = 3^3$.

**Step 3 — Convert the left side.** A power of a power:

$$
9^y = (3^2)^y = 3^{2y}
$$

**Step 4 — Equate the exponents.**

$$
3^{2y} = 3^3 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 2y = 3 \quad\Rightarrow\quad y = \frac{3}{2}
$$

**Check:** $2^5 = 32$ ✓. For $y = \frac{3}{2}$: $9^{3/2}$; we will meet fractional exponents in the Roots section, but $9^{3/2} = (9^{1/2})^3 = 3^3 = 27$ ✓.

**Watch out:** Saying "$27 \div 9 = 3$, so $y = 3$" in $9^y = 27$ is wrong; $9^3 = 729$.

**Answer:** $x = 5$; $y = \dfrac{3}{2}$.
