**What is asked?** A sum of terms with zero and negative exponents.

**Idea:** A negative exponent means "take the reciprocal", and the zeroth power is $1$. Converting each term to a fraction turns the job into adding fractions.

**Step 1 — Convert each term.**

$$
3^{-2} = \frac{1}{3^2} = \frac{1}{9}, \qquad 5^0 = 1, \qquad 2^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}
$$

**Step 2 — The expression.**

$$
\frac{1}{9} + 1 - \frac{1}{2}
$$

**Step 3 — Common denominator.** $\text{LCM}(9, 2) = 18$:

$$
\frac{2}{18} + \frac{18}{18} - \frac{9}{18} = \frac{2 + 18 - 9}{18} = \frac{11}{18}
$$

**Step 4 — Does it simplify?** $11$ is prime and does not divide $18$.

**Watch out:** Writing $3^{-2} = -9$ or $-6$ is the most common slip; a negative exponent does not make the number negative, it takes the reciprocal. $5^0 = 0$ is wrong too: the zeroth power is $1$.

**Answer:** $\dfrac{11}{18}$.
