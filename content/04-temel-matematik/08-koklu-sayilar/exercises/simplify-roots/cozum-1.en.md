**What is asked?** The sum of three roots that look different.

**Idea:** Unlike roots cannot be added directly: $\sqrt{50} + \sqrt{18} \neq \sqrt{68}$. But after simplifying, all three turn out to be multiples of $\sqrt{2}$; then they add like $x$s.

**Step 1 — Simplify.** Take the largest perfect-square factor outside:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{50} &= \sqrt{25 \cdot 2} = 5\sqrt{2} \\
\sqrt{18} &= \sqrt{9 \cdot 2} = 3\sqrt{2} \\
\sqrt{8} &= \sqrt{4 \cdot 2} = 2\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2 — Add.**

$$
5\sqrt{2} + 3\sqrt{2} - 2\sqrt{2} = (5 + 3 - 2)\sqrt{2} = 6\sqrt{2}
$$

$a = 6$.

**Step 3 — The approximate value.**

$$
6 \cdot 1.414 = 8.484 \approx 8.48
$$

With the more precise $\sqrt{2} = 1.414\,21\dots$, $6\sqrt{2} = 8.485\dots \approx 8.49$. Both answers are accepted.

**Watch out:** Writing $\sqrt{50} + \sqrt{18} - \sqrt{8} = \sqrt{60}$ is the most common slip; a root does not spread over a sum. $\sqrt{60} \approx 7.75$, different from the right result.

**Answer:** $a = 6$; about $8.49$.
