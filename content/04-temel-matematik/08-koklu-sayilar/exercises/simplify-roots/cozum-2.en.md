**Idea:** First trap each root between perfect squares and approximate it, then add. This does not give $a$ directly, but it confirms the size of the result, and we can read $a$ from it.

**Step 1 — Approximate roots.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{50} &\approx 7.071 \quad (7^2 = 49) \\
\sqrt{18} &\approx 4.243 \quad (4^2 = 16) \\
\sqrt{8} &\approx 2.828 \quad (3^2 = 9)
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2 — Add.**

$$
7.071 + 4.243 - 2.828 = 8.486 \approx 8.49
$$

**Step 3 — Read off $a$.** $8.486 \div 1.414 \approx 6.00$: the result is $6$ times $\sqrt{2}$.

**Why the same result?** $7.071 = 5 \cdot 1.414$, $4.243 = 3 \cdot 1.414$, $2.828 = 2 \cdot 1.414$: the decimal values carry the same multiples of $\sqrt{2}$. Simplifying showed this common factor at the start, so it gave the exact result ($6\sqrt{2}$); this route gives only the approximate value.

**Answer:** $6$ and $8.49$.
