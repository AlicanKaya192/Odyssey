**Idea:** Write the bases as powers of primes; then multiply the exponents with the power-of-a-power rule. Fractional exponents were defined to fit this rule exactly.

**Step 1 — $8^{2/3}$.** $8 = 2^3$:

$$
(2^3)^{2/3} = 2^{3 \cdot \frac{2}{3}} = 2^2 = 4
$$

**Step 2 — $27^{1/3}$.** $27 = 3^3$:

$$
(3^3)^{1/3} = 3^{1} = 3
$$

**Step 3 — $16^{-1/2}$.** $16 = 2^4$:

$$
(2^4)^{-1/2} = 2^{-2} = \frac{1}{4}
$$

**Why the same result?** A fractional exponent is another way of saying "take the root, then the power". With the base written as a prime power, taking a root turns into dividing the exponent (the cube root of $2^3$ is $2^{3/3}$); both routes do the same calculation in different languages.

**Answer:** $7$ and $\dfrac{1}{4}$.
