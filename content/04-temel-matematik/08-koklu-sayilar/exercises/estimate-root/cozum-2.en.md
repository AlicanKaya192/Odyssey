**Idea:** $200$ contains a large perfect square: $100$. Taking it outside reduces the job to multiplying the known $\sqrt{2}$ by $10$.

**Step 1 — Simplify.**

$$
\sqrt{200} = \sqrt{100 \cdot 2} = 10\sqrt{2}
$$

**Step 2 — Use $\sqrt{2}$.** $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414\,2$:

$$
10 \cdot 1.414\,2 = 14.142
$$

**Step 3 — Read it off.** The whole part is $14$; rounded to two places, $14.14$.

**Why the same result?** The square of $10\sqrt{2}$ is $100 \cdot 2 = 200$. The first route found this number by trying squares; this one saw that it is exactly $10$ times $\sqrt{2}$ and needed a single multiplication. The root rules reduce estimating to a few known roots.

**Answer:** $14$ and $14.14$.
