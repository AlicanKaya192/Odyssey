**Idea:** Since $3 = \sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3}$, we have $6 = 2 \cdot 3 = 2 \cdot \sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3}$. Writing the top this way, the $\sqrt{3}$ in the denominator cancels directly.

**Step 1 — Rewrite the top.**

$$
6 = 2 \cdot \sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3}
$$

**Step 2 — Cancel.**

$$
\frac{2 \cdot \sqrt{3} \cdot \cancel{\sqrt{3}}}{\cancel{\sqrt{3}}} = 2\sqrt{3}
$$

**Why the same result?** Both routes use the fact $\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3} = 3$. The first enlarged the denominator and then simplified; this one found a $\sqrt{3}$ inside the top and cancelled it directly. The general rule comes from here: $\dfrac{a}{\sqrt{a}} = \sqrt{a}$.

**Answer:** $2$ and $3$.
