**What is asked?** Writing a fraction with a root in its denominator in a simple form without that root.

**Idea:** $\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3} = 3$. Multiplying top and bottom by $\sqrt{3}$ makes the denominator a whole number; since we multiply by the same number ($\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}} = 1$), the value does not change.

**Step 1 — Expand.**

$$
\frac{6}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{6 \cdot \sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3}} = \frac{6\sqrt{3}}{3}
$$

**Step 2 — Simplify.** $6 \div 3 = 2$:

$$
\frac{6\sqrt{3}}{3} = 2\sqrt{3}
$$

$a = 2$, $b = 3$.

**Check:** $2\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3} = 2 \cdot 3 = 6$ ✓: $2\sqrt{3}$ really is $6 \div \sqrt{3}$. With decimals: $6 \div 1.732 \approx 3.464$ and $2 \cdot 1.732 = 3.464$ ✓.

**Watch out:** Writing $\frac{6}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{2}$ (cancelling $6$ and $3$ as if both were under the root) is wrong; the $3$ is under the root, the $6$ is not.

**Answer:** $a = 2$, $b = 3$.
