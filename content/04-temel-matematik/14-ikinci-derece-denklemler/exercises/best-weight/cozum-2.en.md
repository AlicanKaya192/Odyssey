**Idea:** Bring $E(w)$ to the form $a(w - h)^2 + k$. Since a square is never negative, the smallest value is at $w = h$ and equals $k$.

**Step 1 — Take out $5$.**

$$
E(w) = 5\left(w^2 - 7.6w\right) + 74
$$

**Step 2 — Complete.** Half of $7.6$ is $3.8$, its square $14.44$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(w) &= 5\left(w^2 - 7.6w + 14.44\right) - 5 \cdot 14.44 + 74 \\
&= 5(w - 3.8)^2 - 72.2 + 74 \\
&= 5(w - 3.8)^2 + 1.8
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3 — Read it off.** $5(w - 3.8)^2 \ge 0$; the smallest value is at $w = 3.8$ and $E = 1.8$.

**Why the same result?** The vertex formula $-\frac{b}{2a}$ is exactly the $h$ that completing the square reveals. This route also gives the smallest value directly, as the constant term.

**Answer:** $3.8$ and $1.8$.
