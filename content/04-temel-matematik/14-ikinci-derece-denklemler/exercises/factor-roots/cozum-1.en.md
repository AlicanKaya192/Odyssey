**What is asked?** The roots of a quadratic equation.

**Idea:** If we factor the expression, the rule "if a product is zero, one factor is zero" gives two linear equations.

**Step 1 — Two numbers.** Product $14$ (positive), sum $-9$ (negative): both negative. $14 = 2 \cdot 7$ and $2 + 7 = 9$: $-2$ and $-7$.

**Step 2 — The factors.**

$$
(x - 2)(x - 7) = 0
$$

**Step 3 — Set each factor to zero.** $x - 2 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 2$; $x - 7 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 7$.

**Check:** Sum $2 + 7 = 9 = -b/a$ ✓, product $14 = c/a$ ✓.

**Watch out:** Writing $(x + 2)(x + 7)$ swaps the signs; then the roots come out as $-2$ and $-7$ and the middle term becomes $+9x$.

**Answer:** $7$ and $2$.
