**What is asked?** The $k$ that gives the equation a single root, and that root.

**Idea:** The discriminant decides the number of roots: when $\Delta = 0$ the two roots coincide and one root remains.

**Step 1 — The discriminant.** $a = 1$, $b = 6$, $c = k$:

$$
\Delta = 36 - 4k
$$

**Step 2 — Set it to zero.** $36 - 4k = 0 \Rightarrow k = 9$.

**Step 3 — The root.** When $\Delta = 0$, $x = -\dfrac{b}{2a} = -\dfrac{6}{2} = -3$.

**Check:** $x^2 + 6x + 9 = (x + 3)^2$: indeed a perfect square with single root $-3$ ✓.

**Reading the result:** For $k < 9$ there are two roots ($\Delta > 0$); for $k > 9$ there is no real root. At $k = 9$ the parabola touches the $x$-axis exactly at $x = -3$.

**Answer:** $k = 9$; the root is $-3$.
