**Idea:** A quadratic with leading coefficient $1$ and single root $r$ has the form $(x - r)^2 = 0$. Which perfect square starts with $x^2 + 6x$?

**Step 1 — The perfect square.** $(x + 3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9$: the middle $6x = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot x$ matches.

**Step 2 — Compare.** For $x^2 + 6x + k = (x + 3)^2$ we need $k = 9$.

**Step 3 — The root.** $(x + 3)^2 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -3$.

**Why the same result?** $\Delta = 0$ means the expression is a perfect square: nothing is left to add when completing the square. The number we add when completing the square, $\left(\frac{6}{2}\right)^2 = 9$, is exactly $k$.

**Answer:** $9$ and $-3$.
