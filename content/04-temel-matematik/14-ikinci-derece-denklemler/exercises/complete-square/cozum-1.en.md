**What is asked?** The roots of an equation that does not factor over whole numbers.

**Idea:** Complete $x^2 - 4x$ to a perfect square: half the coefficient of $x$ is $-2$, its square is $4$. Then take the square root.

**Step 1 — Move the constant right.**

$$
x^2 - 4x = 1
$$

**Step 2 — Add $4$ to both sides.**

$$
x^2 - 4x + 4 = 5 \quad\Rightarrow\quad (x - 2)^2 = 5
$$

**Step 3 — Square root.**

$$
x - 2 = \pm\sqrt{5} \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 2 \pm \sqrt{5}
$$

$p = 2$, $q = 5$.

**Step 4 — The larger root.** $\sqrt{5} \approx 2.236$: $2 + 2.236 = 4.236 \approx 4.24$.

**Check:** The roots add up to $(2 + \sqrt{5}) + (2 - \sqrt{5}) = 4 = -b/a$ ✓; their product is $4 - 5 = -1 = c/a$ ✓.

**Watch out:** Adding the $4$ to the left side only breaks the equation; the right side must become $1 + 4 = 5$.

**Answer:** $p = 2$, $q = 5$; the larger root is $\approx 4.24$.
