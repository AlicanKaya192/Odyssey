**What is asked?** The roots of a quadratic whose leading coefficient is not $1$.

**Idea:** The quadratic formula is $x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}$. Working out the discriminant separately first reduces sign errors.

**Step 1 — Coefficients.** $a = 3$, $b = 5$, $c = -2$.

**Step 2 — The discriminant.**

$$
\Delta = 5^2 - 4 \cdot 3 \cdot (-2) = 25 + 24 = 49
$$

$\Delta > 0$: two roots. $\sqrt{49} = 7$.

**Step 3 — The roots.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_1 &= \frac{-5 + 7}{6} = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3} \\
x_2 &= \frac{-5 - 7}{6} = \frac{-12}{6} = -2
\end{aligned}
$$

**Check:** Sum $\frac{1}{3} - 2 = -\frac{5}{3} = -\frac{b}{a}$ ✓; product $-\frac{2}{3} = \frac{c}{a}$ ✓.

**Watch out:** In $-4ac$, $c$ is negative, so minus times minus is plus: $-4 \cdot 3 \cdot (-2) = +24$. Writing $\Delta = 25 - 24 = 1$ is the most common slip.

**Answer:** $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $-2$.
