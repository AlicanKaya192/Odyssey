**Idea:** Without a formula, write the departure minutes of the two lines side by side and find the common ones. With small numbers this shows plainly what the LCM means.

**Step 1 — Two lists.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{A} &: 0, 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, 72, \dots \\
\text{B} &: 0, 18, 36, 54, 72, \dots
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2 — The first common one.** After $0$, the first number in both lists is $36$. That is the definition of the LCM: the smallest common multiple greater than zero.

**Step 3 — The pattern.** The common ones go $0, 36, 72, \dots$, rising by $36$: once they leave together, everything starts over.

**Step 4 — Convert to clock times.** $08{:}00$, $08{:}36$, $09{:}12$, $09{:}48$, $10{:}24$, $11{:}00$, $11{:}36$. The next, $12{:}12$, is outside the limit. $7$ departures in total.

**Why the same result?** Writing out the lists finds the LCM directly from its definition, "the smallest common multiple". The prime-factor route reaches the same number by a shortcut; as numbers grow, the lists get long and the factor route is preferred.

**Answer:** $36$ and $7$.
