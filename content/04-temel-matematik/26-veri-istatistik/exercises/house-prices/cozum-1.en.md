**What is asked?** The mean and median of skewed data, and the mean once the outlier is gone.

**Idea:** The mean is the total over the count; the median is the middle of the sorted data.

**Step 1 — The mean.** $1.2 + 1.5 + 1.6 + 1.8 + 2.0 + 2.1 + 9.8 = 20$; $\frac{20}{7} \approx 2.857$.

**Step 2 — The median.** Sorted, the fourth value is $1.8$.

**Step 3 — The mean without the outlier.** $\frac{20 - 9.8}{6} = \frac{10.2}{6} = 1.7$.

**Check:** Six houses are below $2.1$; a mean of $2.857$ does not describe a typical house in this street. The median ($1.8$) and the mean without the outlier ($1.7$) are close ✓.

**Watch out:** Presenting the mean as the "typical price" is misleading in this street; a single house pulls it up by more than $1$ million.

**Answer:** $2.857$, $1.8$, $1.7$.
