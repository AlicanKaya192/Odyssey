A worked example for each method in the lesson, step by step. Try each question yourself first, then read the solution.

## 1. Type of data

**Question:** What type of data are a postcode, a $1$–$5$ satisfaction
rating in a survey and the floor area of a house?

A postcode looks like a number but is unordered categorical (its mean is
meaningless). The satisfaction rating is ordered categorical. The floor
area is numerical and continuous.

## 2. Three centres

**Question:** What are the mean, median and mode of $4, 8, 6, 5, 3, 8, 9$?

The sum is $43$, the mean $\frac{43}{7} \approx 6.14$. Sorted:
$3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 8, 9$: the median is $6$. The mode is $8$ (twice).

## 3. An even number of values

**Question:** What is the median of $12, 7, 3, 9, 15, 10$?

Sort: $3, 7, 9, 10, 12, 15$. The two middle values are $9$ and $10$: the
median is $9.5$.

## 4. A weighted mean

**Question:** The midterm counts $40$ percent and the final $60$ percent. A
student scored $80$ in the midterm and $90$ in the final. What is the
grade?

$0.4 \cdot 80 + 0.6 \cdot 90 = 32 + 54 = 86$.

## 5. Variance and standard deviation

**Question:** What are the variance and standard deviation of
$1, 3, 5, 7, 9$?

The mean is $5$. The squared deviations are $16, 4, 0, 4, 16$; total $40$.
Variance $\frac{40}{5} = 8$, standard deviation
$\sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.83$.

## 6. Sample variance

**Question:** If the same five values are a sample, what is the variance?

$\frac{40}{5 - 1} = 10$.

## 7. Quartiles and fences

**Question:** What are the quartiles, IQR and outlier fences of
$2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 12, 15$?

The median is $\frac{7 + 8}{2} = 7.5$. Lower half $2, 4, 5, 7$: Q1 $= 4.5$.
Upper half $8, 10, 12, 15$: Q3 $= 11$. IQR $= 6.5$. The fences are
$4.5 - 9.75 = -5.25$ and $11 + 9.75 = 20.75$; there are no outliers.

## 8. The effect of an outlier

**Question:** How do the mean and median of $10, 12, 14$ change when $100$
is added?

Before: mean $12$, median $12$. After: mean $\frac{136}{4} = 34$, median
$\frac{12 + 14}{2} = 13$. The mean rose by $22$, the median by $1$.

## 9. Z-scores

**Question:** In a measurement with mean $50$ and standard deviation $8$,
what are the z-scores of $62$ and $42$?

$\frac{62 - 50}{8} = 1.5$ and $\frac{42 - 50}{8} = -1$.

## 10. Min–max scaling

**Question:** A column ranges from $20$ to $80$. What does $65$ become after
scaling?

$\frac{65 - 20}{80 - 20} = \frac{45}{60} = 0.75$.
