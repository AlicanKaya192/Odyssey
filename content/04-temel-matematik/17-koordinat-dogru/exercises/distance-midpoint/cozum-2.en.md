**Idea:** Going from $A$ to $B$ takes $8$ steps right and $6$ up. For the distance it is enough to spot a known triple, and for the midpoint to walk half of this path.

**Step 1 — A familiar triple.** $6$ and $8$ are twice the $3$-$4$-$5$ triple. The hypotenuse is twice too: $2 \cdot 5 = 10$.

**Step 2 — Half the path.** Start at $A$ and go $8 / 2 = 4$ right and $6 / 2 = 3$ up: $(-3 + 4, \ 1 + 3) = (1, 4)$.

**Why the same result?** The $3$-$4$-$5$ triple exists because $3^2 + 4^2 = 5^2$; multiplying every side by $2$ keeps the equality, so the same $10$ comes out without a square root. Walking half the path is the equality $\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2} = x_1 + \frac{x_2 - x_1}{2}$ itself.

**Answer:** $10$ and $(1, 4)$.
