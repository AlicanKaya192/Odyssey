**What is asked?** The slope, the $y$-intercept and the $x$-intercept of a line with two known points.

**Idea:** The slope comes from the two points; the slope and one point fix the line. For the $x$-intercept, put $y = 0$.

**Step 1 — The slope.**

$$
m = \frac{-3 - 5}{3 - (-1)} = \frac{-8}{4} = -2
$$

**Step 2 — $b$.** Put in the point $(-1, 5)$: $5 = -2 \cdot (-1) + b = 2 + b$, so $b = 3$. The line is $y = -2x + 3$.

**Step 3 — The $x$-intercept.** $0 = -2x + 3$, $x = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5$.

**Check:** The other point: $-2 \cdot 3 + 3 = -3$ ✓. Both points are on the line.

**Watch out:** On the bottom $3 - (-1) = 4$; writing $2$ gives a slope of $-4$, and then the second point does not fit.

**Answer:** $m = -2$, $b = 3$, $x = 1.5$.
