**What is asked?** The equation of a line perpendicular to a given line and passing through a given point.

**Idea:** The slopes of perpendicular lines multiply to $-1$. First the given line's slope, then the perpendicular slope, then the point–slope form.

**Step 1 — The given line's slope.** $3y = 2x + 6$, that is, $y = \frac{2}{3}x + 2$. The slope is $\frac{2}{3}$.

**Step 2 — The perpendicular slope.** $m = -\frac{3}{2}$, because $\frac{2}{3} \cdot \left( -\frac{3}{2} \right) = -1$.

**Step 3 — The line.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
y - 1 &= -\tfrac{3}{2}(x - 4) \\
y &= -\tfrac{3}{2}x + 6 + 1 = -\tfrac{3}{2}x + 7
\end{aligned}
$$

**Check:** $(4, 1)$: $-\frac{3}{2} \cdot 4 + 7 = -6 + 7 = 1$ ✓. The slopes multiply to $-1$ ✓.

**Watch out:** For the perpendicular slope, only changing the sign ($-\frac{2}{3}$) or only flipping ($\frac{3}{2}$) is not enough; both are needed.

**Answer:** $m = -\frac{3}{2}$, $b = 7$.
