**Idea:** The vertical line from the top down to the $x$-axis ($x = 3$) splits the triangle into two right triangles. Each has legs lined up with the axes, so their areas are easy.

**Step 1 — The corners.** The intersection is $(3, 4)$; the $x$-axis crossings are $(-1, 0)$ and $(5, 0)$ (as in the first solution).

**Step 2 — The left triangle.** Corners $(-1, 0)$, $(3, 0)$, $(3, 4)$: legs $4$ and $4$, area $\frac{4 \cdot 4}{2} = 8$.

**Step 3 — The right triangle.** Corners $(3, 0)$, $(5, 0)$, $(3, 4)$: legs $2$ and $4$, area $\frac{2 \cdot 4}{2} = 4$.

**Step 4 — The total.** $8 + 4 = 12$.

**Why the same result?** Both right triangles have the same height ($4$), and their bases add up to the big triangle's base ($4 + 2 = 6$). $\frac{4 \cdot 4}{2} + \frac{2 \cdot 4}{2} = \frac{6 \cdot 4}{2}$: the distributive property.

**Answer:** $(3, 4)$ and $12$.
