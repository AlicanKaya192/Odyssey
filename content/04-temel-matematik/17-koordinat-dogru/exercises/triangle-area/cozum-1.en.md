**What is asked?** Where the two lines meet, and the area of the triangle bounded by these lines and the $x$-axis.

**Idea:** The triangle's three corners are the intersection of the two lines and the point where each line crosses the $x$-axis. Since the base lies on the $x$-axis, the height is the top corner's $y$.

**Step 1 — The intersection.** $x + 1 = -2x + 10$, $3x = 9$, $x = 3$; $y = 4$. The top is $(3, 4)$.

**Step 2 — The $x$-axis crossings.** $x + 1 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -1$. $-2x + 10 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 5$.

**Step 3 — The area.** Base $5 - (-1) = 6$, height $4$:

$$
A = \frac{6 \cdot 4}{2} = 12
$$

**Check:** The top is on both lines: $3 + 1 = 4$ ✓, $-6 + 10 = 4$ ✓.

**Watch out:** The base is not $5 - 1 = 4$; the left corner is at $-1$, so the length is $6$.

**Answer:** Intersection $(3, 4)$, area $12$.
