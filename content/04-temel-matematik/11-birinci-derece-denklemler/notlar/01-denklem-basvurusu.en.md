## The basic rule

The same operation on both sides: $+k$, $-k$, $\cdot k$, $\div k$ ($k \neq
0$). The equality is kept.

## Order of solving

1. Open brackets (spread a minus over every term).
2. Clear fractions by multiplying by the LCM of the denominators (multiply **every** term).
3. Collect the terms with the unknown on one side, numbers on the other.
4. Divide by the coefficient.
5. Check in the original equation.

## Special cases

| At the end | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $x$ equals a single number | one solution |
| something false like $0 = 5$ | no solution |
| something true like $0 = 0$ | every number is a solution (identity) |

## Rearranging formulas

| Formula | Rearranged |
|---|---|
| $v = \dfrac{d}{t}$ | $t = \dfrac{d}{v}$, $d = vt$ |
| $F = \dfrac{9}{5}C + 32$ | $C = \dfrac{5}{9}(F - 32)$ |
| $y = wx + b$ | $x = \dfrac{y - b}{w}$ ($w \neq 0$) |
| $z = \dfrac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$ | $x = \mu + z\sigma$ |

## Word-problem patterns

| Sentence | Equation |
|---|---|
| "5 more than 3 times a number is 20" | $3x + 5 = 20$ |
| "the two plans cost the same" | plan 1 expression $=$ plan 2 expression |
| "two consecutive numbers add up to 41" | $x + (x + 1) = 41$ |

## Practical tips

- Collect the unknown on the side with the larger coefficient; you avoid negative coefficients.
- With decimals, multiply both sides by $10$ or $100$.
- Put the result not only into the equation but into the **question**: $x = 4.5$ for "how many people?" is a warning sign.
