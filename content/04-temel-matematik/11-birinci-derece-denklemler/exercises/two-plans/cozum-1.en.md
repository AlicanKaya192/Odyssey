**What is asked?** The usage at which two linear prices become equal, and the bill at that point.

**Idea:** Each plan's bill is a linear expression in the usage. The sentence "equal bills" becomes an equation setting the two expressions equal.

**Step 1 — Define the unknown.** $x$ = monthly usage in GB.

**Step 2 — The expressions.** Plan A: $60 + 1.5x$. Plan B: $30 + 4x$.

**Step 3 — Set equal and solve.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
60 + 1.5x &= 30 + 4x \\
30 &= 2.5x \\
x &= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 4 — The bill.** Plan A: $60 + 1.5 \cdot 12 = 60 + 18 = 78$. Plan B: $30 + 4 \cdot 12 = 78$ ✓.

**Reading the result:** Below $12$ GB, B is cheaper (lower fixed fee); above it, A is cheaper (lower price per GB). For example at $20$ GB, A costs $90$ and B $110$.

**Watch out:** Looking only at the fixed fees and saying "B is always cheaper" is wrong; the price per GB closes the gap as usage grows.

**Answer:** $12$ GB; $78$.
