**Idea:** Before multiplying, split the fractions into their terms: $\dfrac{x - 1}{2} = \dfrac{x}{2} - \dfrac{1}{2}$. Then the fractions with $x$ and the constant fractions are added separately.

**Step 1 — Split.**

$$
\frac{x}{2} - \frac{1}{2} + \frac{x}{3} + \frac{2}{3} = 5
$$

**Step 2 — The fractions with $x$.** $\dfrac{x}{2} + \dfrac{x}{3} = \dfrac{3x + 2x}{6} = \dfrac{5x}{6}$.

**Step 3 — The constants.** $-\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{-3 + 4}{6} = \dfrac{1}{6}$.

**Step 4 — Solve.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{5x}{6} + \frac{1}{6} = 5 \\
\Rightarrow\; &\frac{5x}{6} = \frac{29}{6} \\
\Rightarrow\; &x = \frac{29}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Why the same result?** Both routes use the same common denominator ($6$); the first multiplied the whole equation by $6$ at the start, this one added the fractions over the common denominator. In the end $5x + 1 = 30$ and $\frac{5x}{6} + \frac{1}{6} = 5$ are two ways of writing the same equation.

**Answer:** $\dfrac{29}{5}$.
