# Linear Equations

An **expression** ($3x + 4$) describes a value; an **equation** ($3x + 4 =
19$) asks a question: which number $x$ makes the two sides equal? In a
linear equation the unknown appears at most to the first power ($x$, but no
$x^2$). These are the equations solved most often in mathematics: getting
an unknown out of a formula, finding when two price plans cost the same,
or working out a model's decision boundary all come down to this.

Prerequisite: Algebraic Expressions and Identities, Fractions.

**The basic rule:** doing **the same operation** to both sides of an
equation keeps it true. Adding, subtracting, multiplying or dividing by a
non-zero number.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x + 4 &= 19 \\
3x &= 15 &&\text{(subtract } 4 \text{ from both sides)} \\
x &= 5 &&\text{(divide both sides by } 3\text{)}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Check:** $3 \cdot 5 + 4 = 19$ ✓. At the end of every solution, put your
number into the **original** equation.

The shortcut "move it across and change the sign" comes from this rule:
"moving" the $4$ in $3x + 4 = 19$ means subtracting $4$ from both sides.

## The general route

1. Open brackets and simplify each side separately.
2. If there are fractions, multiply both sides by the LCM of the denominators.
3. Collect the terms with the unknown on one side and the numbers on the other.
4. Divide by the coefficient of the unknown.
5. Check by putting it into the original equation.

**The unknown on both sides:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
5x - 7 &= 2x + 8 \\
3x - 7 &= 8 &&\text{(subtract } 2x \text{ from both sides)} \\
3x &= 15 \\
x &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

**With brackets:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
2(x - 3) &= 4 - (x + 1) \\
2x - 6 &= 3 - x \\
3x &= 9 \\
x &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

The minus on the right changed the sign of **both** terms:
$-(x + 1) = -x - 1$.

**With fractions:** multiplying by the LCM of the denominators clears the
fractions. **Every** term must be multiplied.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x}{3} + \frac{x}{4} &= 7 \\
4x + 3x &= 84 &&\text{(multiply both sides by } 12\text{)} \\
7x &= 84 \\
x &= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

## Equations with no solution or infinitely many

If the unknown cancels out, there are two cases:

| Equation | After simplifying | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| $2x + 1 = 2x + 5$ | $1 = 5$ | no $x$ works: **no solution** |
| $2(x + 1) = 2x + 2$ | $2 = 2$ | every $x$ works: it is an **identity** |

## Getting an unknown out of a formula

The same rules work with letters. From the speed formula $v = \dfrac{d}{t}$,
get the time:

$$
v = \frac{d}{t} \quad\Rightarrow\quad vt = d \quad\Rightarrow\quad t = \frac{d}{v}
$$

Temperature: if $F = \dfrac{9}{5}C + 32$, then

$$
F - 32 = \frac{9}{5}C \quad\Rightarrow\quad C = \frac{5}{9}(F - 32)
$$

For $F = 212$, $C = \frac{5}{9} \cdot 180 = 100$ ✓ (the boiling point of
water).

## Turning word problems into equations

1. Give the unknown a letter and **write down** what it stands for.
2. Translate the sentences into mathematics one by one.
3. Solve, and check that the result answers the question.

**Example:** one phone plan costs $40$ a month plus $2$ per GB; another
costs $25$ plus $5$ per GB. At how many GB do the two plans cost the same?

$x$ = number of GB. $40 + 2x = 25 + 5x \Rightarrow 15 = 3x \Rightarrow x =
5$. Below $5$ GB the second plan is cheaper; above it, the first.

## Linear equations in machine learning

**A decision boundary.** A classifier says "yes" when $0.8x - 2$ is
positive and "no" when it is negative. The boundary is where the
expression is zero:

$$
0.8x - 2 = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 2.5
$$

**Undoing scaling.** Data is often scaled with $z = \dfrac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$
($\mu$ the mean, $\sigma$ the spread). To turn the model's output back into
real units we get $x$ out: $x = \mu + z\sigma$.

      <p>$2x + 6 = 10 \Rightarrow x + 6 = 5$</p>
      <p>$\dfrac{x}{3} + 1 = 5 \Rightarrow x + 1 = 15$</p>
      <p>$5 - (x - 2) = 1 \Rightarrow 5 - x - 2 = 1$</p>
    </div>
    <div class="ok">
      <h4>Right</h4>
      <p>$3x = 19 - 4 = 15$</p>
      <p>$x + 3 = 5$ (every term divided by $2$)</p>
      <p>$x + 3 = 15$ (every term multiplied by $3$)</p>
      <p>$5 - x + 2 = 1$</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <figcaption>An operation on both sides applies to <b>every term</b> on both sides.</figcaption>
</figure>

- **Moving a term without changing its sign.** If you forget the shortcut,
  go back to the rule: subtract the same thing from both sides.
- **Dividing by the unknown.** Dividing $x^2 = 3x$ by $x$ loses the solution
  $x = 0$. Linear equations never need it, but do not get into the habit.
- **Skipping the check.** Especially with fractions, slips are easy; put your
  number into the original equation.

## Summary

- An equation is a question asking which value makes both sides equal.
- Do the same operation to both sides: add, subtract, multiply or divide by a non-zero number.
- Order: open brackets, clear fractions with the LCM, collect terms, divide by the coefficient, check.
- If the unknown disappears: something false like $1 = 5$ means no solution; something true like $2 = 2$ means every number is a solution.
- On a graph, the solution is the $x$ where two lines cross.
- The same rules work in formulas: $v = \dfrac{d}{t} \Rightarrow t = \dfrac{d}{v}$.
