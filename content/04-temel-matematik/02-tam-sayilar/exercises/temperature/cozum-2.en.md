**Idea:** Without working out intermediate temperatures, we can add all the changes at once and add them to the start. For the range we use distance on the number line.

**Step 1 — The total change.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
5 \cdot 4 + 3 \cdot (-3) &= 20 + (-9) \\
&= 11
\end{aligned}
$$

Over the day the temperature rose by a net $11$ degrees.

**Step 2 — The final temperature.** $-8 + 11 = 3$.

**Step 3 — The range as a distance.** The highest temperature is when the rise ends: $-8 + 20 = 12$. The distance on the number line between the highest and the lowest:

$$
|12 - (-8)| = |20| = 20
$$

Seen another way: from the lowest to the highest, the temperature rose by exactly the morning rise, $20$ degrees.

**Why the same result?** Addition is associative, so adding the changes one at a time or all together gives the same result. The range is the difference of two temperatures; since the lowest is at the start, that difference is the morning rise itself.

**Answer:** $3$ and $20$.
