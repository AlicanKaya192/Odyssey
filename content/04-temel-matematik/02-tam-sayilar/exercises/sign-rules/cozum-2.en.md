**Idea:** See each term as a product or quotient, and count the sign in front of a term as a factor of $(-1)$. Then count the minuses in each term: an even number gives $+$, an odd number $-$.

**Step 1 — Turn the signs in front into factors.**

$$
(-4)(-3) \; + \; (-1) \cdot 5 \cdot (-2) \; + \; (-18) \div (-6)
$$

**Step 2 — Count the minuses in each term.**

| Term | Absolute value | Minuses | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| $(-4)(-3)$ | $12$ | $2$ (even) | $+12$ |
| $(-1) \cdot 5 \cdot (-2)$ | $10$ | $2$ (even) | $+10$ |
| $(-18) \div (-6)$ | $3$ | $2$ (even) | $+3$ |

**Step 3 — Add.** $12 + 10 + 3 = 25$.

**Why the same result?** A $-$ in front of a term means "take the opposite", that is, multiply by $(-1)$. Writing it as a factor turns the rule "minus times minus" and the rule "subtracting a negative is adding its positive" into the same rule.

**Answer:** $25$.
