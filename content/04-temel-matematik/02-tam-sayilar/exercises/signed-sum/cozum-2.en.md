**Idea:** In addition, order does not matter (commutative and associative). First write all the terms with their signs, then add the positives together and the negatives together. In the end a single addition with different signs remains.

**Step 1 — Write the terms with their signs.**

$$
-7, \quad +12, \quad +5, \quad -20
$$

**Step 2 — The positives.** $12 + 5 = 17$.

**Step 3 — The negatives.** $-7 + (-20) = -27$.

**Step 4 — Combine.** $17 + (-27)$: different signs, $27 - 17 = 10$, and the larger is $-27$:

$$
17 + (-27) = -10
$$

**Reading the result:** Think of money: $17$ owed to you, $27$ you owe; net, you owe $10$.

**Why the same result?** Rearranging the terms of a sum does not change it. The first method added the terms in order, this one grouped them; both add the same four terms.

**Answer:** $-10$.
