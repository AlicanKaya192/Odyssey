**Idea:** $A$ and $B$ are on the two sides of zero. Split the path at zero: from $A$ to zero, and from zero to $B$. For the midpoint, use half the sum of the two numbers.

**Step 1 — Two pieces.** From $-7$ to $0$ is $7$ units, from $0$ to $5$ is $5$ units:

$$
7 + 5 = 12
$$

**Step 2 — Midpoint: the average.** The exact middle of two numbers is half their sum:

$$
\frac{-7 + 5}{2} = \frac{-2}{2} = -1
$$

**Reading the result:** The midpoint is negative because $A$ is further from zero ($7$ units) than $B$ ($5$ units); the midpoint sits just left of zero.

**Why the same result?** The midpoint is $\tfrac{B - A}{2}$ to the right of $A$: $A + \tfrac{B - A}{2} = \tfrac{A + B}{2}$. The first method did this step in two parts, this one in a single formula.

**Answer:** $12$ and $-1$.
