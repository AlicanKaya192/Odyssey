**What is asked?** The fraction of a repeating decimal, the sum of an infinite series, and the ratio of a series with known sum.

**Idea:** For $-1 < r < 1$, $\sum = \frac{a_1}{1 - r}$, where $a_1$ is the first term of the series.

**Step 1 — The repeating decimal.** Two digits repeat: $a_1 = \frac{45}{100}$, $r = \frac{1}{100}$.

$$
\frac{45/100}{99/100} = \frac{45}{99} = \frac{5}{11}
$$

**Step 2 — The series.** The first term is the value at $k = 1$: $a_1 = \frac{2}{5}$, $r = \frac{2}{5}$.

$$
\frac{2/5}{1 - 2/5} = \frac{2/5}{3/5} = \frac{2}{3}
$$

**Step 3 — The ratio.** $\frac{6}{1 - r} = 9$, so $1 - r = \frac{2}{3}$ and $r = \frac{1}{3}$.

**Check:** $5 \div 11 = 0.4545\dots$ ✓. $6 + 2 + \frac{2}{3} + \dots = \frac{6}{2/3} = 9$ ✓.

**Watch out:** Writing $\frac{1}{1 - 2/5} = \frac{5}{3}$ in the second question adds an extra $1$, as if the counter started at $0$.

**Answer:** $\frac{5}{11}$, $\frac{2}{3}$, $\frac{1}{3}$.
