**What is asked?** The values of a function's inverse at two points.

**Idea:** $f$ multiplies the input by $3$ and subtracts $4$. The inverse undoes this: it adds $4$ first, then divides by $3$. The formal route: write $y = f(x)$ and get $x$ out.

**Step 1 — Get $x$ out.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
&y = 3x - 4 \\
\Rightarrow\; &y + 4 = 3x \\
\Rightarrow\; &x = \frac{y + 4}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

$f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{x + 4}{3}$.

**Step 2 — The values.**

$$
f^{-1}(11) = \frac{15}{3} = 5, \qquad f^{-1}(-1) = \frac{3}{3} = 1
$$

**Check:** $f(5) = 15 - 4 = 11$ ✓, $f(1) = 3 - 4 = -1$ ✓.

**Watch out:** Working out $f^{-1}(11)$ as $\dfrac{1}{f(11)} = \dfrac{1}{29}$ treats the superscript as an exponent.

**Answer:** $f^{-1}(11) = 5$, $f^{-1}(-1) = 1$.
