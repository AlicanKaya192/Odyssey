**Idea:** Work out the composite functions in general, then put in $x = 2$. If many values will be needed, this route is handier.

**Step 1 — $g(f(x))$.** Put $f(x)$ inside $g$:

$$
g(2x + 1) = (2x + 1)^2 - 1 = 4x^2 + 4x
$$

**Step 2 — $x = 2$.** $16 + 8 = 24$.

**Step 3 — $f(g(x))$.**

$$
f(x^2 - 1) = 2(x^2 - 1) + 1 = 2x^2 - 1
$$

**Step 4 — $x = 2$.** $8 - 1 = 7$.

**Why the same result?** The composite formula does the same one-after-the-other for a general $x$. That the two formulas differ ($4x^2 + 4x$ and $2x^2 - 1$) shows for every $x$ why the order matters.

**Answer:** $24$ and $7$.
