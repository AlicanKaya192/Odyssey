**What is asked?** The composition of two functions in two different orders.

**Idea:** Composition means connecting two machines in a row. The inner function takes the input, and its output becomes the outer one's input.

**Step 1 — $g(f(2))$: $f$ first.** $f(2) = 2 \cdot 2 + 1 = 5$.

**Step 2 — Then $g$.** $g(5) = 25 - 1 = 24$.

**Step 3 — $f(g(2))$: $g$ first.** $g(2) = 4 - 1 = 3$.

**Step 4 — Then $f$.** $f(3) = 6 + 1 = 7$.

**Reading the result:** The same two functions give results as different as $24$ and $7$ when the order changes. Composition is not commutative.

**Watch out:** Taking $g(f(2))$ to be $g(2) \cdot f(2)$ or $g(2) + f(2)$ is a common slip; composition is not multiplication or addition but applying one after the other.

**Answer:** $g(f(2)) = 24$, $f(g(2)) = 7$.
