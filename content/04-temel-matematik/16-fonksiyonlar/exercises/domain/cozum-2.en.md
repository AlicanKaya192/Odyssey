**Idea:** Try a few inputs and see where the function "breaks". This is the intuitive way of finding the conditions.

**Step 1 — $x = 2$.** $\sqrt{-1}$: not a real number. It breaks.

**Step 2 — $x = 3$.** $\sqrt{0} + \frac{1}{-2}$: defined. The root's limit is exactly $3$.

**Step 3 — $x = 4$, $x = 6$.** Both defined ($1 - 1 = 0$ and $\sqrt{3} + 1$).

**Step 4 — $x = 5$.** $\sqrt{2} + \frac{1}{0}$: the denominator is zero. It breaks.

**Why the same result?** Trying values helps find the conditions but is not a proof on its own; the limits we found ($x = 3$ and $x = 5$) are exactly the two conditions of the first route. Writing the conditions algebraically guarantees that no number in between is missed.

**Answer:** $3$ and $5$.
