**Idea:** By the product–sum rule, $x^2 - 3x + 2 = (x - 1)(x - 2)$. Substituting into the factored form takes fewer steps.

**Step 1 — The factors.** Product $2$, sum $-3$: $-1$ and $-2$. $f(x) = (x - 1)(x - 2)$.

**Step 2 — $f(-2)$.** $(-2 - 1)(-2 - 2) = (-3)(-4) = 12$.

**Step 3 — $f(3)$.** $(3 - 1)(3 - 2) = 2 \cdot 1 = 2$.

**Why the same result?** Two ways of writing the same function give the same value at every input; factoring does not change the function, only its form. A bonus: the factored form shows at once that $f(1) = f(2) = 0$.

**Answer:** $12$ and $2$.
