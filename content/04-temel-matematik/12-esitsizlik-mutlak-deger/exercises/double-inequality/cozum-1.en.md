**What is asked?** The whole numbers in the solution range of a double inequality.

**Idea:** A double inequality is two inequalities written together; doing the same operation on all three parts leaves $x$ alone in the middle. Then we count the whole numbers, paying attention to whether the ends are included.

**Step 1 — Add $1$.**

$$
-6 < 3x \le 12
$$

**Step 2 — Divide by $3$** (positive, no flip):

$$
-2 < x \le 4
$$

**Step 3 — The whole numbers.** $-2$ excluded, $4$ included:

$$
-1, \; 0, \; 1, \; 2, \; 3, \; 4
$$

That is $6$.

**Step 4 — The sum.** $-1 + 0 + 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 9$.

**Check:** $x = -1$: $3(-1) - 1 = -4$, $-7 < -4 \le 11$ ✓. $x = -2$: $-7 < -7$ is false ✓ (excluded). $x = 4$: $11 \le 11$ ✓. $x = 5$: $14 \le 11$ is false ✓.

**Watch out:** Mixing up the ends and including $-2$, or leaving $4$ out, changes both the count and the sum.

**Answer:** $6$ whole numbers; their sum is $9$.
