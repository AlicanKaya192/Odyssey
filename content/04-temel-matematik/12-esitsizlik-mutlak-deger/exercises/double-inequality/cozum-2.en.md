**Idea:** Split the double inequality into two separate ones, solve each, and take the numbers that satisfy both (the intersection).

**Step 1 — The left inequality.** $-7 < 3x - 1 \Rightarrow -6 < 3x \Rightarrow x > -2$.

**Step 2 — The right inequality.** $3x - 1 \le 11 \Rightarrow 3x \le 12 \Rightarrow x \le 4$.

**Step 3 — Both at once.** $x > -2$ **and** $x \le 4$: $-2 < x \le 4$.

**Step 4 — Count and add.** The whole numbers from $-1$ to $4$: $4 - (-1) + 1 = 6$ of them. The sum of consecutive numbers is (first + last) $\cdot$ count $\div 2$:

$$
\frac{(-1 + 4) \cdot 6}{2} = 9
$$

**Why the same result?** A double inequality is two conditions joined by "and"; doing the same operation on all three parts solves both conditions at once. This route solved them separately and intersected.

**Answer:** $6$ and $9$.
