**Idea:** There is no need to expand the whole product. The coefficient of a power comes from the pairs of terms whose product gives that power.

**Step 1 — Pairs giving $x^2$.** $x \cdot x$ or $1 \cdot x^2$:

$$
3x \cdot (-2x) + 1 \cdot x^2 = -6x^2 + x^2 = -5x^2
$$

**Step 2 — Pairs giving $x$.** $x \cdot 1$ or $1 \cdot x$:

$$
3x \cdot 5 + 1 \cdot (-2x) = 15x - 2x = 13x
$$

**Step 3 — The sum.** The sum of the coefficients is the value at $x = 1$; without expanding, with the factors: $(3 + 1) \cdot (1 - 2 + 5) = 16$.

**Why the same result?** Every term of the expansion comes from one pair of factors; we picked only the pairs that produce the power we want. For the sum we applied the $P(1)$ shortcut to each factor separately, because $P(1) = A(1) \cdot B(1)$.

**Answer:** $-5$, $13$ and $16$.
