## Methods

| Method | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| Graph | to see the idea | where two lines cross |
| Substitution | an unknown is alone or has coefficient $1$ | isolate it, put it into the other equation |
| Elimination | coefficients are easy to match | multiply, then add or subtract |

## Elimination steps

1. Make the coefficients of one unknown equal or opposite (multiply the equations).
2. Subtract if equal, add if opposite.
3. Solve the remaining equation in one unknown.
4. Put the value into one of the equations to find the other unknown.
5. Check in both equations.

## Number of solutions

For $ax + by = c$ and $dx + ey = f$:

| Case | Graph | In algebra |
|---|---|---|
| one solution | crossing lines | a value comes out |
| no solution | parallel lines | $0 = k$, $k \neq 0$ |
| infinitely many | the same line | $0 = 0$ |

A quick test: if $\dfrac{a}{d} \neq \dfrac{b}{e}$ there is exactly one solution.

## Word-problem pattern

| Information | Equation |
|---|---|
| total count | $x + y = N$ |
| total value | $p x + q y = T$ |
| "one is twice the other" | $x = 2y$ |
| "they differ by 5" | $x - y = 5$ |

## Practical tips

- With fractional or decimal coefficients, turn the equations into whole numbers first.
- When multiplying an equation, do not forget the right-hand side.
- Write the solution as a pair: $(x, y)$.
