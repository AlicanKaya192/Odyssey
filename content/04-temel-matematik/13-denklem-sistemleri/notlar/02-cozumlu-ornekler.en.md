A worked example for each method in the lesson. Try the question yourself first, then read the solution.

## 1. Adding directly

**Question:** Solve $x + y = 9$ and $x - y = 1$.

Add: $2x = 10$, $x = 5$; $y = 4$. The solution is $(5, 4)$.

## 2. Substitution

**Question:** Solve $x = 3y$ and $2x + y = 21$.

$2(3y) + y = 21$, $7y = 21$, $y = 3$; $x = 9$.

## 3. Isolating first

**Question:** Solve $x + 2y = 8$ and $3x - y = 3$.

From the second, $y = 3x - 3$. Into the first: $x + 6x - 6 = 8$, $7x = 14$,
$x = 2$; $y = 3$.

## 4. Elimination by multiplying

**Question:** Solve $2x + 3y = 13$ and $3x - 2y = 0$.

Multiply the first by $2$ and the second by $3$: $4x + 6y = 26$, $9x - 6y =
0$. Add: $13x = 26$, $x = 2$; $3 \cdot 2 = 2y$, $y = 3$.

## 5. No solution

**Question:** Does $2x - y = 3$ and $4x - 2y = 10$ have a solution?

Multiply the first by $2$: $4x - 2y = 6$. Subtract from the second: $0 = 4$.
False: **no solution**, the lines are parallel.

## 6. Infinitely many

**Question:** $x + 3y = 6$ and $2x + 6y = 12$?

The second is twice the first: the same line, **infinitely many solutions**.

## 7. A word problem

**Question:** Two numbers add up to $50$ and differ by $12$. What are they?

$x + y = 50$, $x - y = 12$: $x = 31$, $y = 19$.

## 8. A line through two points

**Question:** The line $y = wx + b$ passes through $(2, 3)$ and $(6, 11)$.
What are $w$ and $b$?

$2w + b = 3$, $6w + b = 11$. Subtract: $4w = 8$, $w = 2$; $b = -1$.
