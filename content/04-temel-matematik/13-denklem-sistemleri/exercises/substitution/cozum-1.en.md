**What is asked?** The solution of a system where one unknown is already isolated.

**Idea:** The first equation says "$y$ equals $3x - 5$". Writing this wherever $y$ appears in the second equation leaves an equation in one unknown.

**Step 1 — Substitute.**

$$
2x + (3x - 5) = 15
$$

**Step 2 — Solve.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
&5x - 5 = 15 \\
\Rightarrow\; &5x = 20 \\
\Rightarrow\; &x = 4
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3 — $y$.** $y = 3 \cdot 4 - 5 = 7$.

**Check:** $7 = 12 - 5$ ✓; $8 + 7 = 15$ ✓.

**Watch out:** Stopping at $x = 4$, or working out $y$ from the wrong equation without checking, are common slips; the solution is a pair and must work in both equations.

**Answer:** $x = 4$, $y = 7$.
