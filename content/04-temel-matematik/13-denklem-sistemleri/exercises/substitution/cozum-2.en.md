**Idea:** Write the first equation as $-3x + y = -5$; now both equations have coefficient $1$ for $y$. Subtracting removes $y$.

**Step 1 — Rearrange.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
-3x + y &= -5 \\
2x + y &= 15
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2 — Subtract the first from the second.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
&2x - (-3x) = 15 - (-5) \\
\Rightarrow\; &5x = 20 \\
\Rightarrow\; &x = 4
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3 — $y$.** $2 \cdot 4 + y = 15$, $y = 7$.

**Why the same result?** Substitution and elimination do the same job in a different order: both remove $y$ and reach $5x = 20$. When an unknown is already alone, substitution is shorter.

**Answer:** $(4, 7)$.
