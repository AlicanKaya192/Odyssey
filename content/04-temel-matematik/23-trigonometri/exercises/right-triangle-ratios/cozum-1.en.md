**What is asked?** The hypotenuse of a triangle with two known legs, and two ratios of angle $A$.

**Idea:** First the hypotenuse with Pythagoras, then the definitions of the ratios.

**Step 1 — The hypotenuse.** $c = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10$.

**Step 2 — $\sin A$.** Opposite $8$, hypotenuse $10$: $\sin A = \frac{8}{10} = \frac{4}{5}$.

**Step 3 — $\tan A$.** Opposite $8$, adjacent $6$: $\tan A = \frac{8}{6} = \frac{4}{3}$.

**Check:** $\cos A = \frac{6}{10} = \frac{3}{5}$; $\sin^2 A + \cos^2 A = \frac{16}{25} + \frac{9}{25} = 1$ ✓. Also $\frac{\sin A}{\cos A} = \frac{4/5}{3/5} = \frac{4}{3}$ ✓.

**Watch out:** Opposite and adjacent are relative to $A$: $8$ faces $A$. For the other acute angle the roles swap and $\tan$ becomes $\frac{3}{4}$.

**Answer:** $10$, $\frac{4}{5}$, $\frac{4}{3}$.
