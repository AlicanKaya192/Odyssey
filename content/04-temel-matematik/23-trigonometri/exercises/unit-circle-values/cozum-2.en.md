**Idea:** On the unit circle the point at angle $\theta$ is $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$. Find the point by symmetry and read its coordinates.

**Step 1 — $240°$.** The point at $60°$ is $\left( \frac{1}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)$. $240° = 60° + 180°$: half a turn further, the reflection through the centre, $\left( -\frac{1}{2}, -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)$. $\sin 240°$ is the second coordinate, $\approx -0.866$.

**Step 2 — $300°$.** $300° = -60°$: the reflection of the $60°$ point in the $x$-axis, $\left( \frac{1}{2}, -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)$. $\cos 300° = \frac{1}{2}$.

**Step 3 — $135°$.** The reflection of the $45°$ point in the $y$-axis, $\left( -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)$. $\tan = \frac{y}{x} = -1$.

**Why the same result?** The reference angle method is the shortcut for these symmetries: reflections keep the size of the coordinates and change only their signs.

**Answer:** $-0.866$, $\frac{1}{2}$ and $-1$.
