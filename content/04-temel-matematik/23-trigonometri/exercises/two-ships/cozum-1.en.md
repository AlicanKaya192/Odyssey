**What is asked?** The third side of a triangle with two known sides and the angle between them, and the cosine of one of its angles.

**Idea:** In a triangle that is not right-angled, the law of cosines replaces Pythagoras: $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$.

**Step 1 — $60°$.** $\cos 60° = \frac{1}{2}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
c^2 &= 64 + 25 - 2 \cdot 8 \cdot 5 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \\
&= 89 - 40 = 49, \quad c = 7
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2 — $120°$.** $\cos 120° = -\frac{1}{2}$:

$$
c^2 = 89 + 40 = 129, \quad c = \sqrt{129} \approx 11.358
$$

**Step 3 — $\cos B$.** The sides are $5$, $7$, $8$; for the angle facing $8$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
64 &= 25 + 49 - 2 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 \cos B \\
70 \cos B &= 10 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \cos B = \tfrac{1}{7}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Check:** With an obtuse angle the distance must be larger than with an acute one: $11.36 > 7$ ✓. $\cos B > 0$, so $B$ is acute; even the angle facing the longest side is acute, so the whole triangle is acute.

**Watch out:** $\cos 120°$ is negative; if the sign slips, the second question also gives $7$.

**Answer:** $7$ km, $\approx 11.358$ km, $\cos B = \frac{1}{7}$.
