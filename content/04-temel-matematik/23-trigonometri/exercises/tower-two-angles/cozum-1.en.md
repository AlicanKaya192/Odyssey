**What is asked?** The height of a tower seen from two points, and the distances.

**Idea:** Each observation gives a right triangle; both share the same $h$. If the second point is $x$ away, the first is $x + 20$.

**Step 1 — Two equations.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
h &= x \tan 60° = \sqrt{3}\, x \\
h &= (x + 20) \tan 30° = \frac{x + 20}{\sqrt{3}}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2 — Set equal.** $\sqrt{3}\, x = \frac{x + 20}{\sqrt{3}}$; multiply both sides by $\sqrt{3}$: $3x = x + 20$, $x = 10$.

**Step 3 — The results.** $h = 10\sqrt{3} \approx 17.32$ m. The second point is $10$ m away and the first $30$ m.

**Check:** $\tan 30° \cdot 30 = \frac{30}{\sqrt{3}} = 10\sqrt{3}$ ✓, the same height.

**Watch out:** The $20$ m is the **difference** of the two distances; the first point is $x + 20$ away, not $20$.

**Answer:** $17.32$ m, $10$ m, $30$ m.
