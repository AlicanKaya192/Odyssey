**Idea:** The two observation points and the top of the tower form a triangle. Looking at its angles shows it is isosceles.

**Step 1 — The angles.** The angle at the first point is $30°$. The interior angle at the second point is $180° - 60° = 120°$. The angle at the top is $180° - 30° - 120° = 30°$.

**Step 2 — Isosceles.** Two angles are $30°$, so the sides facing them are equal: the distance from the second point to the top equals the path walked, $20$ m.

**Step 3 — A right triangle.** From the second point: hypotenuse $20$, angle $60°$. Height $20 \sin 60° = 10\sqrt{3} \approx 17.32$; horizontal distance $20 \cos 60° = 10$. The first point is $10 + 20 = 30$ m away.

**Why the same result?** $60°$ is an exterior angle: the sum of the two interior angles facing it, $30° + 30°$. This equality is the geometric form of the equation $3x = x + 20$ in the first solution.

**Answer:** $17.32$, $10$ and $30$.
