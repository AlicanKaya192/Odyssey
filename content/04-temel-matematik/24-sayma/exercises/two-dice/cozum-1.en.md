**What is asked?** The probabilities of three events with two dice.

**Idea:** All $36$ ordered outcomes are equally likely. The probability is the number of matching outcomes over $36$.

**Step 1 — Sum $8$.** $(2, 6)$, $(3, 5)$, $(4, 4)$, $(5, 3)$, $(6, 2)$: $5$ outcomes. $P = \frac{5}{36}$.

**Step 2 — At least one $6$.** No $6$ at all: $5$ options per die, $25$ outcomes. At least one $6$: $36 - 25 = 11$. $P = \frac{11}{36}$.

**Step 3 — The same number.** $(1, 1)$, …, $(6, 6)$: $6$ outcomes. $P = \frac{6}{36} = \frac{1}{6}$.

**Check:** Count at least one $6$ directly: red $6$ ($6$ outcomes) plus blue $6$ ($6$ outcomes) minus both ($1$): $11$ ✓.

**Watch out:** $(3, 5)$ and $(5, 3)$ are different outcomes; the dice can be told apart. Counting them as one makes the outcomes unequally likely and gives a wrong answer.

**Answer:** $\frac{5}{36}$, $\frac{11}{36}$, $\frac{1}{6}$.
