**Idea:** Think of the dice in turn: first red, then blue. The multiplication principle applies to probabilities too.

**Step 1 — Sum $8$.** If red shows $1$ the sum cannot be $8$; if it shows $2$ to $6$ ($\frac{5}{6}$), exactly one value of blue works ($\frac{1}{6}$): $\frac{5}{6} \cdot \frac{1}{6} = \frac{5}{36}$.

**Step 2 — At least one $6$.** Neither is $6$: $\frac{5}{6} \cdot \frac{5}{6} = \frac{25}{36}$. The opposite: $1 - \frac{25}{36} = \frac{11}{36}$.

**Step 3 — The same.** Whatever red shows, the probability that blue matches it is $\frac{1}{6}$.

**Why the same result?** Multiplying probabilities is the same as multiplying outcome counts and dividing by $36$: $\frac{5}{6} \cdot \frac{1}{6} = \frac{5 \cdot 1}{36}$. Thinking step by step counts without writing the table.

**Answer:** $\frac{5}{36}$, $\frac{11}{36}$ and $\frac{1}{6}$.
