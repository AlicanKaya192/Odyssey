**What is asked?** Using the difference-of-squares pattern with both numbers and letters.

**Idea:** $a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$. Instead of working out two squares, we multiply the difference by the sum; with well-chosen numbers this is much easier.

**Step 1 — Difference and sum.** $53 - 47 = 6$, $53 + 47 = 100$.

**Step 2 — Multiply.**

$$
53^2 - 47^2 = 6 \cdot 100 = 600
$$

**Step 3 — $4x^2 - 81$.** Both terms are perfect squares: $4x^2 = (2x)^2$, $81 = 9^2$.

$$
4x^2 - 81 = (2x - 9)(2x + 9)
$$

$a = 9$.

**Check:** $53^2 = 2\,809$, $47^2 = 2\,209$; the difference is $600$ ✓. $(2x - 9)(2x + 9) = 4x^2 - 81$ ✓.

**Watch out:** Writing $4x^2 - 81 = (4x - 9)(x + 9)$ without taking the root of the coefficient breaks the pattern; the square root of $4x^2$ is $2x$.

**Answer:** $600$ and $a = 9$.
