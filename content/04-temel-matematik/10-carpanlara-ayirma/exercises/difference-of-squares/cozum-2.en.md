**Idea:** The average of $53$ and $47$ is $50$; both are $3$ away from it. Expanding the squares as $(50 + 3)^2$ and $(50 - 3)^2$, most terms cancel.

**Step 1 — Expand.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
(50 + 3)^2 &= 2\,500 + 300 + 9 \\
(50 - 3)^2 &= 2\,500 - 300 + 9
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2 — Subtract.** The $2\,500$s and $9$s go; what is left is $300 - (-300) = 600$.

**Step 3 — The second question.** The same logic: $(2x - a)(2x + a) = 4x^2 - a^2$. For this to be $4x^2 - 81$ we need $a^2 = 81$, $a = 9$.

**Why the same result?** What is left in the expansion, $2 \cdot 2 \cdot 50 \cdot 3 = 600$, is exactly $(53 - 47)(53 + 47) = 6 \cdot 100$. The difference-of-squares identity does this cancelling once, in general, and keeps it.

**Answer:** $600$ and $9$.
