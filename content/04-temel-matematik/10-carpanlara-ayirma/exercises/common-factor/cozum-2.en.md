**Idea:** If you cannot see the greatest common factor at once, take out the small common factors you do see, one after another; keep going until nothing common is left inside.

**Step 1 — First $x^2$.** $12x^3 - 18x^2 = x^2(12x - 18)$.

**Step 2 — Then $2$.** $12x - 18 = 2(6x - 9)$: $x^2 \cdot 2(6x - 9)$.

**Step 3 — Then $3$.** $6x - 9 = 3(2x - 3)$: $x^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3(2x - 3) = 6x^2(2x - 3)$.

**Step 4 — When to stop.** In $2x - 3$, $2$ and $3$ share no factor, and $x$ is in only one term: done.

**Why the same result?** As with prime factorisation, whatever order you take the factors out in, the result is the same: $x^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 = 6x^2$. The first route found it in one step with the GCD.

**Answer:** $6$, $2$, $2$ and $-3$.
