**What is asked?** Taking the greatest common factor out of a two-term expression.

**Idea:** The common factor has two parts: the GCD of the coefficients and, for each letter, the smallest power present in both terms. The inside of the bracket is each term divided by that factor.

**Step 1 — Coefficients.** $12 = 2^2 \cdot 3$, $18 = 2 \cdot 3^2$; GCD $= 2 \cdot 3 = 6$.

**Step 2 — The letter.** What $x^3$ and $x^2$ share is $x^2$ (the smaller exponent).

**Step 3 — The common factor.** $6x^2$. So $k = 6$, $n = 2$.

**Step 4 — Inside the bracket.**

$$
\frac{12x^3}{6x^2} = 2x, \qquad \frac{-18x^2}{6x^2} = -3
$$

$$
12x^3 - 18x^2 = 6x^2(2x - 3)
$$

$a = 2$, $b = -3$.

**Check:** $6x^2 \cdot 2x = 12x^3$, $6x^2 \cdot (-3) = -18x^2$ ✓.

**Watch out:** $3x(4x^2 - 6x)$ or $6x(2x^2 - 3x)$ are true but unfinished: there is still a common factor inside the bracket.

**Answer:** $k = 6$, $n = 2$, $a = 2$, $b = -3$.
