**What is asked?** Simplifying an algebraic fraction and evaluating it at a value.

**Idea:** In a fraction only **factors** cancel. First factor the top and the bottom, then cancel the common factor.

**Step 1 — The top.** A difference of squares:

$$
x^2 - 4 = (x - 2)(x + 2)
$$

**Step 2 — The bottom.** Product $-6$, sum $1$: $3$ and $-2$.

$$
x^2 + x - 6 = (x + 3)(x - 2)
$$

**Step 3 — Cancel.**

$$
\frac{(x - 2)(x + 2)}{(x + 3)(x - 2)} = \frac{x + 2}{x + 3}
$$

$p = 2$, $q = 3$. (The equality holds except at $x = 2$ and $x = -3$.)

**Step 4 — $x = 7$.**

$$
\frac{7 + 2}{7 + 3} = \frac{9}{10}
$$

**Check:** With the original fraction: $\dfrac{49 - 4}{49 + 7 - 6} = \dfrac{45}{50} = \dfrac{9}{10}$ ✓.

**Watch out:** Cancelling the $x^2$s in $\dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x^2 + x - 6}$ to get $\dfrac{-4}{x - 6}$ is wrong; $x^2$ is a term, not a factor.

**Answer:** $p = 2$, $q = 3$; the value is $\dfrac{9}{10}$.
