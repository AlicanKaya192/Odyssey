**What is asked?** Factoring a trinomial with leading coefficient $1$ into two factors.

**Idea:** $(x + p)(x + q) = x^2 + (p + q)x + pq$. Comparing coefficients, we need $pq = -15$ and $p + q = -2$.

**Step 1 — Signs.** The product is negative: one positive, one negative. The sum is negative: the one with the larger absolute value is negative.

**Step 2 — Factor pairs of $15$.** $1 \cdot 15$ and $3 \cdot 5$. The pair that differs by $2$: $3$ and $5$.

**Step 3 — Give signs.** The larger is negative: $-5$ and $3$. Check: $(-5) \cdot 3 = -15$, $-5 + 3 = -2$ ✓.

**Step 4 — Write it.**

$$
x^2 - 2x - 15 = (x - 5)(x + 3)
$$

Since $p < q$: $p = -5$, $q = 3$.

**Check:** $(x - 5)(x + 3) = x^2 + 3x - 5x - 15 = x^2 - 2x - 15$ ✓.

**Watch out:** Writing $(x + 5)(x - 3)$ swaps the signs; expanding gives $+2x$ in the middle.

**Answer:** $p = -5$, $q = 3$.
