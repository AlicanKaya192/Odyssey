**Idea:** $(x + p)(x + q)$ is zero at $x = -p$ and $x = -q$. If we find the $x$ values that make the expression zero by trying, the factors follow from them.

**Step 1 — Candidates.** A whole-number zero must divide the constant term ($-15$): $\pm 1, \pm 3, \pm 5, \pm 15$.

**Step 2 — Try.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
x = 5 &: \; 25 - 10 - 15 = 0 \; ✓ \\
x = -3 &: \; 9 + 6 - 15 = 0 \; ✓
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3 — The factors.** If $x = 5$ is a zero, $(x - 5)$ is a factor; if $x = -3$, then $(x + 3)$. Their product starts with $x^2$, as the expression does:

$$
x^2 - 2x - 15 = (x - 5)(x + 3)
$$

**Why the same result?** If a product is zero, one factor is zero; conversely, for every $x = r$ that makes the expression zero, $(x - r)$ is a factor. The first route reached the factors from the coefficients, this one from the zeros.

**Answer:** $-5$ and $3$.
