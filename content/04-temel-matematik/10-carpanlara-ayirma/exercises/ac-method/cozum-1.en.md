**What is asked?** Factoring a trinomial whose leading coefficient is not $1$.

**Idea:** The $ac$ method: multiply the coefficient of $x^2$ by the constant ($6 \cdot (-2) = -12$). Split the middle term with two numbers whose product is $-12$ and whose sum is the middle coefficient ($1$); that gives four terms, which we group and factor.

**Step 1 — Two numbers.** Product $-12$, sum $1$: $4$ and $-3$.

**Step 2 — Split the middle term.**

$$
6x^2 + x - 2 = 6x^2 + 4x - 3x - 2
$$

**Step 3 — Group.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
6x^2 + 4x - 3x - 2 &= 2x(3x + 2) - 1(3x + 2) \\
&= (2x - 1)(3x + 2)
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 4 — Read off.** In the order with $a < c$: $(2x - 1)(3x + 2)$. $a = 2$, $b = -1$, $c = 3$, $d = 2$.

**Check:** $(2x - 1)(3x + 2) = 6x^2 + 4x - 3x - 2 = 6x^2 + x - 2$ ✓.

**Watch out:** In the second group, $-3x - 2 = -1(3x + 2)$; taking the minus out changes the sign of $-2$ too. Writing $-1(3x - 2)$ loses the common factor.

**Answer:** $a = 2$, $b = -1$, $c = 3$, $d = 2$.
