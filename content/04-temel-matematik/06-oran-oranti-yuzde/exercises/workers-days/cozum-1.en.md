**What is asked?** How the time changes when the number of workers changes, and how many workers a given time needs.

**Idea:** If every worker does the same amount each day, the size of the job is measured in "worker-days": the number of workers times the number of days. The job is fixed, so this product is fixed: inverse proportion.

**Step 1 — The size of the job.**

$$
6 \cdot 10 = 60 \text{ worker-days}
$$

**Step 2 — With $4$ workers.**

$$
4 \cdot x = 60 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 15 \text{ days}
$$

**Step 3 — To finish in $5$ days.**

$$
n \cdot 5 = 60 \quad\Rightarrow\quad n = 12 \text{ workers}
$$

**Reading the result:** When the workers go $6 \to 4$ (to two thirds), the days go $10 \to 15$ (the reciprocal, $\frac{3}{2}$ times). To halve the time ($10 \to 5$), the workers must double ($6 \to 12$).

**Watch out:** Setting up a direct proportion, $\frac{6}{10} = \frac{4}{x}$, gives $x \approx 6.7$ days: fewer workers finishing sooner, which makes no sense. Check the direction of the relationship first.

**Answer:** $15$ days; $12$ workers.
