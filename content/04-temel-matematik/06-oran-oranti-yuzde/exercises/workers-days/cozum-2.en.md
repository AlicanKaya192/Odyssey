**Idea:** Count the whole job as $1$. Find the share of the job one worker does in one day; then work out the total daily share in the required situation.

**Step 1 — One worker's daily share.** If $6$ workers do $1$ job in $10$ days, $6$ workers do $\frac{1}{10}$ of it in a day, and one worker in one day does

$$
\frac{1}{10} \div 6 = \frac{1}{60}
$$

**Step 2 — $4$ workers.** $4 \cdot \frac{1}{60} = \frac{1}{15}$ of the job per day. The whole job takes $15$ days.

**Step 3 — Finishing in $5$ days.** $\frac{1}{5}$ of the job must be done per day. How many workers?

$$
n \cdot \frac{1}{60} = \frac{1}{5} \quad\Rightarrow\quad n = \frac{60}{5} = 12
$$

**Why the same result?** "$60$ worker-days" and "one worker's daily share is $\frac{1}{60}$" are the same information. The first method used the total size of the job, this one the unit rate; the constant of the inverse proportion is $60$ in both.

**Answer:** $15$ and $12$.
