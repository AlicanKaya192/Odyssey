**Idea:** Write each change as a factor: a $20\%$ rise is $1.2$, a $20\%$ fall is $0.8$. For successive changes the factors multiply; the net factor gives the net change directly.

**Step 1 — The net factor.**

$$
1.2 \cdot 0.8 = 0.96
$$

**Step 2 — The final price.**

$$
500 \cdot 0.96 = 480
$$

**Step 3 — The net change.** The factor is $0.96 = 1 - 0.04$: a $4\%$ fall, that is, $-4$.

**Why the same result?** Working out $500 \cdot 1.2 \cdot 0.8$ left to right is the first method ($600$, then $480$); working out $1.2 \cdot 0.8$ first is this one. Grouping does not change a product. The advantage of this route: the net change does not depend on the starting price; whatever it was instead of $500$, it falls by $4\%$.

**Answer:** $480$ and $-4$.
