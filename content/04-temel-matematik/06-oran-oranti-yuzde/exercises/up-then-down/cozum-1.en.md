**What is asked?** The price after two successive percentage changes, and the overall change.

**Idea:** Each percentage change is a percentage of the **current** price. In the second step, $20\%$ is of $600$, not of $500$.

**Step 1 — The rise.** $20\%$ of $500$ is $100$:

$$
500 + 100 = 600
$$

**Step 2 — The cut.** $20\%$ of $600$ is $120$:

$$
600 - 120 = 480
$$

**Step 3 — The net change.**

$$
\frac{480 - 500}{500} \cdot 100 = \frac{-20}{500} \cdot 100 = -4
$$

**Reading the result:** The rise was $100$ and the cut $120$, because the cut is a percentage of a larger price. Overall it got $4\%$ cheaper.

**Watch out:** "$+20 - 20 = 0$, so the price is unchanged" is the most common slip. The percentages are of different wholes, so they cannot be added.

**Answer:** $480$; $-4\%$.
