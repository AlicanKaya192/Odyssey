**Idea:** Cut the old price into $100$ equal parts; each part is $1\%$ of the old price. After the discount $85$ parts are left, and they come to $340$.

**Step 1 — Find $1\%$.**

$$
85 \text{ parts} = 340 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 1 \text{ part} = 340 \div 85 = 4
$$

**Step 2 — The old price.** $100$ parts: $100 \cdot 4 = 400$.

**Step 3 — The discount.** $15$ parts: $15 \cdot 4 = 60$.

**Why the same result?** This is the equation $0.85x = 340$ without decimals: we took $1\%$ of $x$ as the unit and said that $85$ units come to $340$. We divided by $85$ instead of $0.85$, then multiplied by $100$; $\frac{340}{85} \cdot 100 = \frac{340}{0.85}$.

**Answer:** $400$ and $60$.
