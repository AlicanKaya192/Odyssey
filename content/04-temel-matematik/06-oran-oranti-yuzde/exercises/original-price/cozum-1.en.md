**What is asked?** The value before a percentage change, when the value after it is known.

**Idea:** The discount is a percentage of the old price, which is unknown. Call it $x$ and write the equality "new = old $\cdot$ factor".

**Step 1 — The factor.** A $15\%$ discount: $1 - 0.15 = 0.85$.

**Step 2 — The equation.**

$$
0.85 \cdot x = 340
$$

**Step 3 — Solve.** Divide both sides by $0.85$. Move the points and work out $34\,000 \div 85$:

$$
x = \frac{340}{0.85} = \frac{34\,000}{85} = 400
$$

**Step 4 — The amount saved.** $400 - 340 = 60$. Check: $15\%$ of $400$ $= 60$ ✓.

**Watch out:** $15\%$ of $340$ is $51$; adding it to get $391$ is wrong, because the $15\%$ is of the unknown old price, not of $340$. Check: $15\%$ of $391$ is $58.65$; $391 - 58.65 = 332.35 \neq 340$.

**Answer:** $400$; the discount was $60$.
