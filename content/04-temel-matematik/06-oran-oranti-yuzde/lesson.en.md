# Ratio, Proportion and Percentages

"Two out of every three people", "$1$ cm on the map is $500$ m on the
ground", "the price rose by $20\%$", "the model is $85\%$ right"… In daily
life and in data we often describe numbers by **comparing them with each
other**. In this section we will see ratio, the two kinds of proportion
and percentages; above all, how percentage changes are worked out and
where they mislead.

Prerequisite: the Fractions and Decimals sections.

## Ratio

The **ratio** of two quantities says how many times one is the other.
With $12$ girls and $8$ boys, the ratio of girls to boys is

$$
12 : 8 = \frac{12}{8} = \frac{3}{2}
$$

that is, $3$ girls for every $2$ boys. A ratio simplifies like a
fraction: $12 : 8 = 3 : 2$.

**Part to part is not part to whole.** Girls to boys is $3 : 2$; but the
girls are $\dfrac{3}{3 + 2} = \dfrac{3}{5}$ of the class. If the ratio is
$a : b$, the whole is $a + b$ parts.

**Sharing an amount in a ratio:** to share $600$ in the ratio $2 : 3$,
the whole is $2 + 3 = 5$ parts and one part is $600 \div 5 = 120$:

$$
2 \cdot 120 = 240, \qquad 3 \cdot 120 = 360
$$

**Unit rate:** if "$5$ kg costs $120$", then $1$ kg costs $120 \div 5 =
24$. The easiest way to compare different packs is the unit price.

## Proportion

An equality of two ratios is called a **proportion**:

$$
\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a \cdot d = b \cdot c
$$

The equality on the right (cross-multiplying) is how we find an unknown.
If $3$ pens cost $7.5$, how much do $8$ pens cost?

$$
\frac{3}{7.5} = \frac{8}{x} \quad\Rightarrow\quad 3x = 8 \cdot 7.5 = 60 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 20
$$

| | Direct proportion | Inverse proportion |
|---|---|---|
| What stays fixed? | the ratio: $\dfrac{y}{x} = k$ | the product: $x \cdot y = k$ |
| When one doubles | the other doubles | the other halves |
| Example | number of items and total price | number of workers and time taken |

**An inverse example:** $6$ workers finish a job in $10$ days. How many
days do $4$ workers take? The size of the job is fixed: $6 \cdot 10 = 60$
"worker-days".

$$
4 \cdot x = 60 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 15 \text{ days}
$$

Fewer workers, more days: the direction we expect ✓.

**How do I tell which it is?** Ask yourself: "When one goes up, does the
other go up or down?" But careful: not every "goes up together"
relationship is direct proportion. If a taxi charges a starting fee plus
a price per kilometre, twice the distance does not cost twice as much;
the ratio is not fixed.

### Scale

Maps and drawings are direct proportion. On a $1 : 50\,000$ map, $1$ cm is
$50\,000$ cm $= 500$ m on the ground. A road that is $7.4$ cm on the map:

$$
7.4 \cdot 50\,000 = 370\,000 \text{ cm} = 3.7 \text{ km}
$$

## Percentages

A **percentage** is a fraction with denominator $100$: $p\% =
\dfrac{p}{100}$. "$35$ per cent" means "$35$ in every $100$".

| Percentage | Fraction | Decimal |
|---|---|---|
| $50\%$ | $\dfrac{1}{2}$ | $0.5$ |
| $25\%$ | $\dfrac{1}{4}$ | $0.25$ |
| $20\%$ | $\dfrac{1}{5}$ | $0.2$ |
| $10\%$ | $\dfrac{1}{10}$ | $0.1$ |
| $12.5\%$ | $\dfrac{1}{8}$ | $0.125$ |
| $150\%$ | $\dfrac{3}{2}$ | $1.5$ |

There are three basic questions, all from the same equality: **part =
percentage · whole**.

| Question | Known | Calculation |
|---|---|---|
| What is $35\%$ of $80$? | percentage, whole | $0.35 \cdot 80 = 28$ |
| $28$ is what percentage of $80$? | part, whole | $28 \div 80 = 0.35 = 35\%$ |
| $35\%$ of which number is $28$? | part, percentage | $28 \div 0.35 = 80$ |

**Percentages in your head:** find $10\%$ (move the point one place), then
multiply. $10\%$ of $80$ is $8$; $30\%$ is $24$; $5\%$ is $4$; $35\%$ is
$28$.

## Percentage change

If a value rises by $p\%$, the new value is $1 + \dfrac{p}{100}$ times
the old one; if it falls by $p\%$, $1 - \dfrac{p}{100}$ times. We can
call this the **growth factor**.

$$
500 \xrightarrow{+20\%} 500 \cdot 1.2 = 600, \qquad 500 \xrightarrow{-15\%} 500 \cdot 0.85 = 425
$$

**The percentage change:**

$$
\text{change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \cdot 100
$$

If a price went from $80$ to $92$: $\dfrac{12}{80} = 0.15$, a $15\%$ rise.
The denominator is always the **old** value.

A $20\%$ rise and a $20\%$ fall do **not** cancel. With factors it is
plain: $1.2 \cdot 0.8 = 0.96$. Adding the percentages ($+20 - 20 = 0$) is
wrong; multiplying the factors is right.

### Reverse percentages

After a $15\%$ discount a product costs $340$. What was the old price? If
the old price is $x$, then $0.85x = 340$:

$$
x = 340 \div 0.85 = 400
$$

Adding $15\%$ of $340$ to get $391$ is wrong: the $15\%$ was a percentage
of the old price, not the new one.

### Percent versus percentage points

A model's error rate fell from $10\%$ to $8\%$. That is:

- a fall of **$2$ percentage points** ($10 - 8$),
- a fall of **$20\%$** ($\dfrac{2}{10}$).

Both are correct but they say different things. In news and reports,
watch which one is meant.

## Ratio and percentages in machine learning

**The split ratio.** Data is split $80 : 20$ into training and testing:
with $5\,000$ examples, $4\,000$ for training and $1\,000$ for testing.

**Accuracy can mislead.** Say $950$ of $1\,000$ emails are normal and
$50$ are spam. A lazy model that calls every email "normal" scores $95\%$
accuracy but never catches a single spam email. When the classes are
very unbalanced, accuracy alone is not enough.

**Relative improvement.** When the error drops from $10\%$ to $8\%$, people
say "the error fell by $20\%$"; that is the relative change. "It fell by
$2$ points" is the absolute change.

- **Adding percentages.** For successive changes, multiply the factors.

- **Treating every increasing relationship as direct proportion.** Check
  whether the ratio is fixed.

## Summary

- Ratio $a : b = \dfrac{a}{b}$; it simplifies. The whole is $a + b$ parts.
- Proportion $\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d} \Leftrightarrow ad = bc$.
- Direct proportion: fixed ratio ($y = kx$). Inverse proportion: fixed product ($xy = k$).
- Scale $1 : n$: map length $\cdot\, n$ = real length.
- $p\% = \dfrac{p}{100}$. Part = percentage $\cdot$ whole.
- A $p\%$ rise: $\cdot (1 + \tfrac{p}{100})$; a fall: $\cdot (1 - \tfrac{p}{100})$. For successive changes, multiply the factors.
- In a percentage change the denominator is the old value. Reverse percentage: find it by dividing.
- Percentage points are an absolute difference; percentage change is relative.
