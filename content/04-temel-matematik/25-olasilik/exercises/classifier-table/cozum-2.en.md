**Idea:** Without building the table, using the addition rule and the definition of conditional probability.

**Step 1 — Saying "cat".** Two disjoint ways: really cat and "cat" ($\frac{70}{200}$), or really dog and "cat" ($\frac{20}{200}$): $\frac{90}{200} = 0.45$.

**Step 2 — Accuracy.** Correct "cat" ($\frac{70}{200}$) and correct "dog" ($\frac{120 - 20}{200} = \frac{100}{200}$): $0.85$.

**Step 3 — Conditional.** $P(\text{cat} \mid \text{"cat"}) = \frac{P(\text{cat and "cat"})}{P(\text{"cat"})} = \frac{0.35}{0.45} = \frac{7}{9}$.

**Why the same result?** The formula's $\frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(A)}$ is "cell over column total" in the table. Both use the same numbers; one as proportions, the other as counts.

**Answer:** $0.45$, $0.85$ and $\frac{7}{9}$.
