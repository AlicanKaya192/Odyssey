**What is asked?** The probabilities of the union and intersection of two events and of one complement.

**Idea:** The outcomes are equally likely; write the events as sets and count.

**Step 1 — The sets.** $A = \{2, 3, 5\}$, $B = \{4, 5, 6\}$.

**Step 2 — The union.** $A \cup B = \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$: $\frac{5}{6}$.

**Step 3 — Intersection and complement.** $A \cap B = \{5\}$: $\frac{1}{6}$. $A' = \{1, 4, 6\}$: $\frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$.

**Check:** With the union rule $\frac{3}{6} + \frac{3}{6} - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$ ✓.

**Watch out:** $1$ is not a prime; primes have exactly two divisors. Putting $1$ into $A$ would make the union $1$.

**Answer:** $\frac{5}{6}$, $\frac{1}{6}$, $\frac{1}{2}$.
