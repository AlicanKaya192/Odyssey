**Idea:** The first two questions are counted with unordered choices. The third has a symmetry: the drawing order is only a label.

**Step 1 — Both red.** $\frac{\binom{4}{2}}{\binom{10}{2}} = \frac{6}{45} = \frac{2}{15}$.

**Step 2 — Different colours.** $\frac{\binom{4}{1}\binom{6}{1}}{\binom{10}{2}} = \frac{24}{45} = \frac{8}{15}$.

**Step 3 — Second red.** Imagine lining the balls up in a random order; the ball in second place is just as "random" as the one in first place. The probability that the ball in any given place is red is $\frac{4}{10} = \frac{2}{5}$.

**Why the same result?** In the tree $\frac{4}{10} \cdot \frac{3}{9} + \frac{6}{10} \cdot \frac{4}{9} = \frac{4 \cdot (3 + 6)}{90} = \frac{4}{10}$: the algebra confirms the symmetry. Without information, the order does not matter.

**Answer:** $\frac{2}{15}$, $\frac{8}{15}$ and $\frac{2}{5}$.
