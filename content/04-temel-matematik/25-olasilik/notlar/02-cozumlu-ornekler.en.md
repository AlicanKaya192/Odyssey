A worked example for each method in the lesson, step by step. Try each question yourself first, then read the solution.

## 1. The sample space

**Question:** A coin is tossed twice. What is the sample space? What is the
probability of at least one head?

$S = \{TT, TH, HT, HH\}$, four equally likely outcomes. At least one head:
$TH$, $HT$, $HH$, so $\frac{3}{4}$.

## 2. The classical definition

**Question:** One card is drawn from cards numbered $1$ to $20$. What is the
probability that the number is a multiple of $3$?

$3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18$: $6$ cards. $P = \frac{6}{20} = \frac{3}{10}$.

## 3. The complement

**Question:** Four dice are rolled. What is the probability that at least
one shows $6$?

No $6$ at all: $\left( \frac{5}{6} \right)^4 = \frac{625}{1296} \approx
0.482$. At least one $6$: $1 - 0.482 \approx 0.518$.

## 4. A union

**Question:** In a class of $30$, $12$ play football and $10$ basketball;
$4$ play both. Does a randomly chosen student play football or basketball?

$\frac{12 + 10 - 4}{30} = \frac{18}{30} = 0.6$.

## 5. Independent events

**Question:** Two dice are rolled. What is the probability that both show
$6$? That the first is even and the second is greater than $5$?

$\frac{1}{6} \cdot \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{36}$. $\frac{3}{6} \cdot
\frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{12}$.

## 6. Independent or not?

**Question:** $P(A) = 0.5$, $P(B) = 0.4$ and $P(A \cap B) = 0.2$. Are $A$
and $B$ independent?

$0.5 \cdot 0.4 = 0.2 = P(A \cap B)$: yes, independent.

## 7. Drawing without replacement

**Question:** $2$ of $5$ pens are broken. $2$ pens are taken without
replacement; what is the probability that both are broken?

$\frac{2}{5} \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{2}{20} = \frac{1}{10}$.

## 8. Drawing with replacement

**Question:** In the same question, what if the first pen were put back?

$\frac{2}{5} \cdot \frac{2}{5} = \frac{4}{25} = 0.16$; larger than without
replacement, because a broken pen can be chosen twice.

## 9. Conditional probability

**Question:** Two dice add up to $8$. What is the probability that the
first die is $3$?

The outcomes with sum $8$ are $(2, 6), (3, 5), (4, 4), (5, 3), (6, 2)$; one
of them has first die $3$: $\frac{1}{5}$.

## 10. A two-way table

**Question:** Of $200$ students, $120$ studied for an exam. $100$ of those
who studied passed, and $30$ of those who did not. What are
$P(\text{passed})$, $P(\text{passed} \mid \text{studied})$ and
$P(\text{studied} \mid \text{passed})$?

$130$ passed: $\frac{130}{200} = 0.65$. Among those who studied:
$\frac{100}{120} \approx 0.83$. Among those who passed, the share who
studied: $\frac{100}{130} \approx 0.77$.
