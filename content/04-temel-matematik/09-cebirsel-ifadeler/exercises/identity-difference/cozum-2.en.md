**Idea:** The expression is already a difference of two squares: $P^2 - Q^2 = (P - Q)(P + Q)$, with $P = a - b$ and $Q = a + b$. Factoring without expanding is shorter.

**Step 1 — Difference and sum.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
P - Q &= (a - b) - (a + b) = -2b \\
P + Q &= (a - b) + (a + b) = 2a
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2 — Multiply.**

$$
(-2b)(2a) = -4ab
$$

$k = -4$.

**Step 3 — The value.** $-4 \cdot 3 \cdot (-2) = 24$.

**Why the same result?** $(P - Q)(P + Q) = P^2 - Q^2$ is an identity; it holds whatever expressions stand for $P$ and $Q$. The first route expanded inside the squares; this one used the identity one level up without expanding the squares at all.

**Answer:** $-4$ and $24$.
