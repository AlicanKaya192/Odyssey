**What is asked?** Simplifying the difference of two perfect squares, then working out a value.

**Idea:** Expand both squares with the perfect-square identities; in the subtraction, the whole second expansion goes in brackets and is multiplied by minus. Then like terms cancel.

**Step 1 — Expand.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
(a - b)^2 &= a^2 - 2ab + b^2 \\
(a + b)^2 &= a^2 + 2ab + b^2
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2 — Subtract.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
&(a^2 - 2ab + b^2) - (a^2 + 2ab + b^2) \\
&= a^2 - 2ab + b^2 - a^2 - 2ab - b^2
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3 — Like terms.** The $a^2$ and $b^2$ terms go; $-2ab - 2ab = -4ab$. So $k = -4$.

**Step 4 — The value.** $ab = 3 \cdot (-2) = -6$:

$$
-4 \cdot (-6) = 24
$$

**Check:** Directly: $(3 - (-2))^2 - (3 + (-2))^2 = 5^2 - 1^2 = 25 - 1 = 24$ ✓.

**Watch out:** When opening the second bracket, changing only the sign of $a^2$ and leaving $+2ab$ as it is gives a wrong result such as $k = 0$.

**Answer:** $k = -4$; the value is $24$.
