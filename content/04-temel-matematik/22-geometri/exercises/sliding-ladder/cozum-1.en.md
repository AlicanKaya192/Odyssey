**What is asked?** The height of a ladder against a wall in two positions and the difference.

**Idea:** The wall, the ground and the ladder form a right triangle; the ladder is the hypotenuse, always $13$ long.

**Step 1 — The first height.** $h^2 = 13^2 - 5^2 = 169 - 25 = 144$, $h = 12$ m.

**Step 2 — The new height.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
h'^2 &= 169 - 49 = 120 \\
h' &= \sqrt{120} \approx 10.954
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3 — The difference.** $12 - 10.954 = 1.046$ m.

**Check:** $7^2 + 10.954^2 \approx 49 + 120.0 = 169 = 13^2$ ✓.

**Watch out:** The foot slid $2$ m, but the top does not come down $2$ m; the lengths are linked through squares, the relation is not linear.

**Answer:** $12$ m, about $10.95$ m, about $1.05$ m.
