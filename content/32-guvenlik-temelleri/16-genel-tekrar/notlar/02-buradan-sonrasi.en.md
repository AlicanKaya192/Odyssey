This module built the concepts. Three more modules follow on the track;
each one deepens an idea from here.

## Cryptography

Here we used `hashlib` and `hmac` but did not open them up. The
Cryptography module explains how hash functions, symmetric and asymmetric
encryption, digital signatures and certificates work, with small measured
examples. The real form of the signed token from the Identity Management
section is a combination of the topics there.

## Network and Web Security

The largest part of the attack surface is often the network and web
applications. This module covers firewalls, HTTPS and the common web
vulnerabilities listed by OWASP (injection, broken access control, session
mistakes). The principle "the decision is made on the server" from the
Authorization section will come up in every example there.

## System Security and Operations

What happens after the measures are in place: how systems are hardened,
how logs are monitored and what is done when an incident happens. The
"what to do after an infection" order from the Malware section becomes an
incident response plan there; the Detect, Respond and Recover functions of
NIST CSF are the subject of this module too.

## While you study

- Look for the concepts in systems you see: which factors does the login
  of your school or an application you use rely on, which standard does
  its single sign-on use?
- Ask the four questions of threat modelling in your own small projects.
- Try the techniques you learn only on your own systems or in practice
  environments where it is clearly allowed. Trying without permission is a
  crime.
