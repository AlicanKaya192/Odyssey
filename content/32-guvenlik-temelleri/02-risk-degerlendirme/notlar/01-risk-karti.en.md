## Qualitative

- Likelihood 1–5, impact 1–5; **risk score = likelihood × impact** (1–25).
- The levels in this lesson: 1–4 low, 5–9 medium, 10–16 high, 17–25
  critical.
- What the scores mean is written down beforehand; several people score
  together.

## Quantitative

| Short | Name | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| **AV** | Asset value | Estimated |
| **EF** | Exposure factor | Between 0 and 1 |
| **SLE** | Single loss expectancy | AV × EF |
| **ARO** | Annual rate of occurrence | Times per year |
| **ALE** | Annual loss expectancy | SLE × ARO |

**A control's net benefit** = ALE before − ALE after − the control's yearly
cost. If it is negative, the control costs more than the risk.

## Four answers

| Answer | In short |
|---|---|
| **Mitigate** | Take a control |
| **Transfer** | Insurance, an outside service |
| **Avoid** | Do not do that activity |
| **Accept** | Leave it, knowingly and in writing |

## Terms

- **Residual risk:** the risk left after the controls.
- **Risk appetite:** the risk an organisation is ready to accept.
- **Risk register:** the table where every risk is kept with its name,
  score, owner and answer.
- **Risk owner:** the person responsible for that risk; they decide.

## Traps

- A risk with likelihood 1 and impact 5 looks "low" by score; disaster
  scenarios are handled separately.
- A quantitative result looks exact, but its inputs are estimates.
- An accepted risk is not forgotten; it is looked at again regularly.
