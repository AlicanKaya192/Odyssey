## What is a p-value?

The probability of a result at least this extreme if $H_0$ were true.

## What is it not?

| Wrong sentence | Why wrong |
|---|---|
| "The probability that $H_0$ is true" | p is computed **assuming** $H_0$; it cannot give its probability |
| "The probability that the result is due to chance" | Another way of saying the same mistake |
| "A 5% chance that the finding is wrong" | In this section 71% of significant results with 100-person tests were wrong |
| "A small p means a big effect" | In a huge sample a 10-second difference gave p ≈ 1e-7 |
| "p > 0.05 means no effect" | With low power real effects are often missed |
| "If I repeat it, p < 0.05 again" | The same experiment's p wandered between 0.022 and 0.836 |

## Next to p in a report

- The difference and its confidence interval
- The sample size (and the power if possible)
- How many comparisons were made
- Whether the analysis was planned in advance or found afterwards

## Write the exact p

Write "p = 0.032" instead of "p < 0.05". "p = 0.049" and "p = 0.051" are
almost the same evidence; let the reader see that.
