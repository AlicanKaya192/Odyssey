The same result can be stated in two ways, and they leave very different
impressions. In this section's checkout button test:

- **Absolute difference:** 0.118 → 0.140, that is +0.022
  (about 2.2 percentage points).
- **Relative lift:** 18.6% ("sales rose 19%").

## Percent or percentage points?

- **Percentage point**: the difference of two percentages. Going from 11.8%
  to 14.0% is 2.2 percentage points.
- **Percent** (relative): the difference divided by the starting value. The
  same rise is a 19% increase.

Saying "rose by 2.2%" is ambiguous; always state which one you mean.

## A small base rate inflates the relative lift

| Before | After | Absolute | Relative |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1% | 2% | +1 point | 100% |
| 40% | 41% | +1 point | 2.5% |

In both, one more person out of 100 buys. The relative lift makes the first
look spectacular and the second trivial.

## Which one when?

- For **decisions and revenue** use the absolute difference: 2.2 points on
  10,000 users means 220 extra purchases.
- For **comparing different products** the relative lift helps, but write
  the base rate next to it.
- State on which scale you give the confidence interval; the interval of the
  absolute difference cannot be turned directly into one for the relative
  lift (the denominator is uncertain too).

## A common mistake

Making the relative lift the headline of a report without writing the
absolute difference and the base rate. The reader sees "19%", not "2
points".
