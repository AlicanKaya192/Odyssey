In an incident, what people know and when matters as much as the technical
work. If it is unclear who decides, everyone does something; if it is
unclear who is to be informed, the news reaches the wrong person at the
wrong time.

## Roles

| Role | Duty |
|---|---|
| Incident lead | Makes decisions, orders the steps, knows what everyone is doing |
| Technical team | Does the analysis, containment, eradication and recovery |
| Scribe | Writes down every decision and every step with its time |
| Communications lead | Manages what is said inside and outside the organisation |
| Legal and management | Take on legal notifications and business decisions |

In a small team one person may take several roles; what matters is that
the roles are clear **before the incident**.

## The incident log

While the incident lasts, everything is written down: what was seen when,
which decision was made and why, who took which step. This log serves three
purposes: the team does not forget what was done, the post-incident review
rests on real records, and if needed it becomes evidence in a legal
process.

## The communication channel

It is assumed the attacker may have taken over email or the chat system.
That is why a channel set in advance and **independent** of the affected
systems is used to talk during the incident. The channel and the contact
list are prepared in the preparation step; they are not searched for during
the incident.

## Who is told, and when?

- **Inside the organisation:** management and the affected teams,
  according to the priority level.
- **The people affected:** those whose personal data was affected; what
  happened and what they should do (such as changing a password), in plain
  language.
- **Authorities:** in cases such as a personal data breach, laws require
  notification within a set period (the Privacy and Data Protection
  section). These periods are written into the plan.

## What is said outside

A statement made outside about the incident is accurate, measured and
comes from one voice. Something unknown is stated as "we do not know yet,
we are investigating"; a guess is not told as fact. Wrong or contradictory
statements can cause a bigger loss of trust than the incident itself.
