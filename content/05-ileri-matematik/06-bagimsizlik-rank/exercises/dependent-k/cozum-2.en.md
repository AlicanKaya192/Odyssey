**Idea:** Write $\mathbf{v} = c\,\mathbf{u}$ and match components; we find the multiple first, then $k$. This is the route closest to the definition of dependence.

**Step 1 — Match the components.**

$$
(3, 6) = c \cdot (2, k) = (2c,\ ck)
$$

**Step 2 — The first component gives the multiple.** $2c = 3$, so $c = 1.5$.

**Step 3 — The second component gives $k$.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
ck &= 6 \\
1.5\,k &= 6 \\
k &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

**Check:** $1.5 \cdot (2, 4) = (3, 6)$ ✓.

**Why the same result?** The determinant $12 - 3k$ is the condition that the component ratios agree ($\tfrac{2}{3} = \tfrac{k}{6}$), written by cross-multiplying.

**Answer:** $4$ and $1.5$.
