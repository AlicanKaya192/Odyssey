**Idea:** Use the sign of $f'$ to find where the function rises and where it falls. The largest value can only be at "the end of a climb", the smallest at "the end of a descent".

**Step 1 — The sign.** $f' = 3x(x - 2)$: $+$ on $[-1, 0)$, $-$ on $(0, 2)$, $+$ on $(2, 4]$.

**Step 2 — The movement.** It climbs from $-1$ to $0$, descends from $0$ to $2$, and climbs from $2$ to $4$.

**Step 3 — The candidates.** The ends of the climbs: $x = 0$ ($f = 0$) and $x = 4$ ($f = 16$); the largest is $16$. The end of the descent and the starting point: $x = 2$ ($f = -4$) and $x = -1$ ($f = -4$); the smallest is $-4$.

**Why the same result?** Both methods look at the same candidates; this one also shows which candidate competes for which role. $x = 0$ is a local maximum, but the later climb goes past it.

**Answer:** $16$ and $-4$.
