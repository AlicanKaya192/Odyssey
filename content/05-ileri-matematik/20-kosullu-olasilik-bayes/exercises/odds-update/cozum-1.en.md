**What is asked?** The prior odds and the posterior probabilities after two independent pieces of evidence.

**Idea:** In odds form Bayes is a multiplication: each independent piece of evidence multiplies the odds by its likelihood ratio.

**Step 1 — Prior odds.** $\frac{0.2}{0.8} = \frac{1}{4}$.

**Step 2 — The first word.** $\frac{1}{4} \cdot 8 = 2$. Probability $\frac{2}{1 + 2} = \frac{2}{3}$.

**Step 3 — The second word.** $2 \cdot 3 = 6$. Probability $\frac{6}{7} \approx 0.857$.

**Check:** The probability grows with each piece of evidence but never exceeds $1$ ✓.

**Watch out:** Multiplying the probability directly by the likelihood ratio ($0.2 \cdot 8 = 1.6$) goes above $1$; the multiplication is done with **odds**.

**Answer:** $\frac{1}{4}$, $\frac{2}{3}$, $\frac{6}{7}$.
