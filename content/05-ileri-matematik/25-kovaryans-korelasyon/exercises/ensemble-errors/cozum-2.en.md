**Idea:** First find the covariance, then write the variance of the sum, finally multiply by $\frac{1}{4}$.

**Step 1 — $\rho = 0.6$.** $\operatorname{Cov} = 0.6 \cdot 9 = 5.4$. $\operatorname{Var}(e_1 + e_2) = 9 + 9 + 10.8 = 28.8$; $\frac{28.8}{4} = 7.2$.

**Step 2 — $\rho = 0.2$.** $\operatorname{Cov} = 1.8$; $18 + 3.6 = 21.6$; $\frac{21.6}{4} = 5.4$.

**Step 3 — Target.** $\frac{18 + 2c}{4} = 6$ gives $c = 3$; $\rho = \frac{3}{9} \approx 0.333$.

**Why the same result?** $\frac{2\sigma^2 + 2\rho\sigma^2}{4} = \frac{\sigma^2(1 + \rho)}{2}$; the formula is these steps simplified.

**Answer:** $7.2$, $5.4$ and $0.333$.
