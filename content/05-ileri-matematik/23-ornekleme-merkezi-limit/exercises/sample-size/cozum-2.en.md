**Idea:** The standard error is inversely proportional to $\sqrt{n}$; once one value is known the others follow by ratio.

**Step 1 — Start.** $4$ at $n = 36$.

**Step 2 — Halving.** To shrink the error $2$ times, $n$ grows $2^2 = 4$ times: $144$.

**Step 3 — To a quarter.** $576 = 16 \cdot 36$; $\sqrt{16} = 4$, error $\frac{4}{4} = 1$.

**Why the same result?** $\frac{\text{SE}_1}{\text{SE}_2} = \sqrt{\frac{n_2}{n_1}}$ is the formula in ratio form; it works even without knowing $\sigma$.

**Answer:** $4$, $144$ and $1$.
