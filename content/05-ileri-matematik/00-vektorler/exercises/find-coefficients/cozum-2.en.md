The same two equations:

$$
a + 3b = 7 \qquad 2a + b = 9
$$

Multiply the first by $2$: $2a + 6b = 14$. Subtract the second from it; $a$ disappears:

$$
(2a + 6b) - (2a + b) = 14 - 9 \;\Rightarrow\; 5b = 5 \;\Rightarrow\; b = 1
$$

From the first equation $a = 7 - 3 \cdot 1 = 4$.

Elimination stays tidy as the number of unknowns grows; the Gaussian elimination chapter of MATH 2 is this method scaled up.

**Answer: $a = 4$, $b = 1$**
