**Idea:** Posterior odds $=$ prior odds $\times$ likelihood ratio. The likelihood ratio is $\frac{0.9}{0.05} = 18$.

**Step 1 — $P(+)$.** $0.02 \cdot 0.9 + 0.98 \cdot 0.05 = 0.067$.

**Step 2 — One test.** Prior odds $2 : 98$; posterior odds $36 : 98$; probability $\frac{36}{134} \approx 0.269$.

**Step 3 — Two tests.** The odds are multiplied by $18$ once more: $648 : 98$; probability $\frac{648}{746} \approx 0.869$.

**Why the same result?** Writing Bayes' rule as a ratio of two hypotheses cancels $P(+)$; each independent test grows the odds by the same factor. Updating for the two tests one at a time and multiplying the odds twice are the same calculation.

**Answer:** $0.067$, $0.269$ and $0.869$.
