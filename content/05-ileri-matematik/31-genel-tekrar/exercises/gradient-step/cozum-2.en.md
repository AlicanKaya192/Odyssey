**Idea:** $f(x, y) = y(x^2 + 3)$. The product form makes both the derivatives and the value easier.

**Step 1 — $\partial f / \partial x$.** $y$ constant: $y \cdot 2x = 4$.

**Step 2 — $\partial f / \partial y$.** Linear in $y$: its coefficient is $x^2 + 3 = 4$.

**Step 3 — New value.** At $(0.6, \ 1.6)$: $1.6 \cdot (0.36 + 3) = 1.6 \cdot 3.36 = 5.376$.

**Why the same result?** $y(x^2 + 3)$ and $x^2y + 3y$ are the same function; holding the other variable fixed in a partial derivative means treating that factor of the product as a constant coefficient.

**Answer:** $4$, $4$ and $5.376$.
