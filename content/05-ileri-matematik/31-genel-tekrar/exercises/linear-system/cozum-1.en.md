**What is asked?** The solution of a $2 \times 2$ linear system.

**Idea:** If $\det A \neq 0$, the unique solution is $x = A^{-1}b$.

**Step 1 — Determinant.** $3 \cdot 4 - 1 \cdot 2 = 10$.

**Step 2 — Inverse.** $A^{-1} = \frac{1}{10}\begin{pmatrix} 4 & -1 \\ -2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$.

**Step 3 — Product.** $x = \frac{1}{10}\begin{pmatrix} 28 - 8 \\ -14 + 24 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

**Check:** $3 \cdot 2 + 1 = 7$ ✓, $2 \cdot 2 + 4 \cdot 1 = 8$ ✓.

**Watch out:** Writing $bA^{-1}$ instead of $A^{-1}b$; the sizes do not match, the order matters.

**Answer:** $10$, $2$, $1$.
