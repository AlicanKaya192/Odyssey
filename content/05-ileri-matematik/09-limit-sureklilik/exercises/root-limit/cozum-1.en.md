**What is asked?** A limit with a root on top that gives $\frac{0}{0}$ on substitution.

**Idea:** Multiplying $\sqrt{x} - 2$ by its conjugate $\sqrt{x} + 2$ removes the root and brings out the hidden factor $(x - 4)$ on top.

**Step 1 — Multiply by the conjugate.**

$$
\frac{\sqrt{x} - 2}{x - 4} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{x} + 2}{\sqrt{x} + 2} = \frac{x - 4}{(x - 4)(\sqrt{x} + 2)}
$$

**Step 2 — Cancel.** Since $x \neq 4$, $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x} + 2}$.

**Step 3 — Substitute.** $\dfrac{1}{2 + 2} = \dfrac{1}{4}$.

**Check:** $x = 4.01$: $\sqrt{4.01} \approx 2.0025$; $\frac{0.0025}{0.01} = 0.25$ ✓.

**Watch out:** There is no need to multiply out the bottom; left unexpanded, the cancellation shows at once.

**Answer:** $\frac{1}{4}$.
