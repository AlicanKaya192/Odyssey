**Idea:** To get rid of the root, let $t = \sqrt{x}$. Then $x = t^2$, and as $x \to 4$, $t \to 2$.

**Step 1 — Rewrite.**

$$
\frac{\sqrt{x} - 2}{x - 4} = \frac{t - 2}{t^2 - 4}
$$

**Step 2 — Difference of squares.** $t^2 - 4 = (t - 2)(t + 2)$; after cancelling, $\dfrac{1}{t + 2}$.

**Step 3 — The limit.** $t \to 2$: $\dfrac{1}{4}$.

**Why the same result?** Multiplying by the conjugate is really seeing $x - 4$ as the difference of squares $(\sqrt{x} - 2)(\sqrt{x} + 2)$. Writing $t$ makes this factorisation appear by itself.

**Answer:** $0.25$.
