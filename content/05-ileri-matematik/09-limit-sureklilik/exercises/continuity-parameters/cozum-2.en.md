**Idea:** Thinking in pictures: in $f$ the two pieces must meet at the same height at $x = 2$; the graph of $g$ is a line with a single hole, and $k$ is the point that fills it.

**Step 1 — The end of the right piece.** The line $3x - 1$ is at height $5$ at $x = 2$.

**Step 2 — Move the left piece there.** The parabola $x^2$ is at height $4$ at $x = 2$; $a$ shifts it up. To reach $5$ it needs $a = 1$ unit.

**Step 3 — The graph of $g$.** For $x \neq 2$, $g(x) = x + 2$: a line. At $x = 2$ the line has height $4$, and there is a hole there. To fill the hole, $k = 4$.

**Why the same result?** "Meeting at the same height" is the left limit equalling the right limit, and "filling the hole" is the value equalling the limit. The two views state two conditions of the definition of continuity in pictures.

**Answer:** $1$ and $4$.
