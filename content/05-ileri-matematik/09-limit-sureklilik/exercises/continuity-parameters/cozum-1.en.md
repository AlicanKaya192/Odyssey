**What is asked?** The parameter values that make two functions continuous.

**Idea:** Continuity: $f(a)$ defined, the limit exists, the two are equal. There is no problem inside the pieces; only check the joining point.

**Step 1 — The left limit of $f$.** $x \to 2^-$: $x^2 + a \to 4 + a$.

**Step 2 — The right limit and value of $f$.** $x \to 2^+$ and $f(2)$: $3 \cdot 2 - 1 = 5$.

**Step 3 — Set them equal.** $4 + a = 5$, $a = 1$.

**Step 4 — The limit of $g$.** $\frac{(x - 2)(x + 2)}{x - 2} = x + 2 \to 4$. The value must equal the limit: $k = 4$.

**Check:** $a = 1$: on the left, $x = 1.99$ gives $1.99^2 + 1 = 4.9601$; on the right, $x = 2.01$ gives $5.03$. Both are close to $5$ ✓.

**Watch out:** Trying to compute $g(2)$ from the formula gives $\frac{0}{0}$; $g(2)$ is defined by $k$, not by the formula.

**Answer:** $a = 1$, $k = 4$.
