**What is asked?** Two limits that give $\frac{0}{0}$ on substitution.

**Idea:** $\frac{0}{0}$ says the top is also zero at $x = a$, so it carries the factor $(x - a)$. Separating and cancelling that factor leaves an expression we can substitute into.

**Step 1 — The first limit.**

$$
\frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3} = \frac{(x - 3)(x + 3)}{x - 3} = x + 3 \;\to\; 6
$$

**Step 2 — The second limit.** Product $6$, sum $-5$: $-2$ and $-3$.

$$
\frac{x^2 - 5x + 6}{x - 2} = \frac{(x - 2)(x - 3)}{x - 2} = x - 3 \;\to\; -1
$$

**Check:** $x = 2.01$: $\frac{4.0401 - 10.05 + 6}{0.01} = \frac{-0.0099}{0.01} = -0.99$. Close to $-1$ ✓.

**Watch out:** Before cancelling, remember that $x \neq a$; the simplified and original expressions differ only at $x = a$, and the limit does not look at exactly that point.

**Answer:** $6$ and $-1$.
