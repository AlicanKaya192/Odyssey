Draw the two vectors from the same point; the side joining their tips is $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$. Look at the lengths of the three sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|\mathbf{a}\| &= \sqrt{2} \\
\|\mathbf{b}\| &= \sqrt{2} \\
\|\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}\| &= \|(0,\ 1,\ -1)\| = \sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

All three sides are equal: this is an **equilateral triangle**. Every angle of an equilateral triangle is $60°$, so the angle between $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ is $60°$ too.

No cosine computed, only lengths. This is the same triangle we used in the lesson to prove the geometric meaning of the dot product.

**Answer: $60°$**
