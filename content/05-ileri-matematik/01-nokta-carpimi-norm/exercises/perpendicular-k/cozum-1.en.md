**What is asked?** The first component of $\mathbf{a}$ is unknown. For which value of $k$ is the angle between $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ exactly $90°$?

**Idea:** The dot product of two perpendicular vectors is zero, because

$$
\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \|\mathbf{a}\|\,\|\mathbf{b}\|\cos 90° = 0
$$

Writing the dot product in terms of $k$ and setting it to zero gives an equation with one unknown.

**Step 1 — Write the dot product in terms of $k$.** Multiply matching components and add:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} &= k \cdot 4 + 3 \cdot (-2) \\
&= 4k - 6
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2 — Set it to zero and solve.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
4k - 6 &= 0 \\
4k &= 6 \\
k &= \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5
\end{aligned}
$$

**Check:** With $k = 1.5$, is the dot product really zero?

$$
(1.5,\ 3) \cdot (4,\ -2) = 1.5 \cdot 4 + 3 \cdot (-2) = 6 - 6 = 0
$$

Zero, so the vectors are perpendicular. ✓

**Watch out:** Miss the minus in $3 \cdot (-2)$ and you get $4k + 6 = 0$, $k = -1.5$; the check catches this at once.

**Answer:** $k = 1.5$.
