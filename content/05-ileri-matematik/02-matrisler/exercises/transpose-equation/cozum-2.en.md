With the rules $(A + B)^\mathsf{T} = A^\mathsf{T} + B^\mathsf{T}$ and $(A^\mathsf{T})^\mathsf{T} = A$ you can transpose both sides of the equation. The matrix with the unknowns loses its transpose:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} x & 2 \\ y & 5 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 3 & z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 2 \\ 7 & 9 \end{bmatrix}
$$

Now entry by entry:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + 1 &= 4 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 3 \\
y + 3 &= 7 \;\Rightarrow\; y = 4 \\
5 + z &= 9 \;\Rightarrow\; z = 4
\end{aligned}
$$

The same equations; this time the transpose was applied to the known matrices. The transpose rules let you bring an equation into the form that is easiest to read.

**Answer: $x = 3$, $y = 4$, $z = 4$**
