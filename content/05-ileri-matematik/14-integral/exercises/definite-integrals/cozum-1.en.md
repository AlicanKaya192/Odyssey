**What is asked?** The value of two definite integrals.

**Idea:** Find an antiderivative and subtract its value at the lower limit from its value at the upper limit.

**Step 1 — The first integral.**

$$
\big[x^3 - x^2\big]_1^3 = (27 - 9) - (1 - 1) = 18
$$

**Step 2 — The second integral.** The antiderivative of $x^{-1/2}$ is $2x^{1/2}$:

$$
\big[2\sqrt{x}\big]_1^4 = 4 - 2 = 2
$$

**Check:** $(x^3 - x^2)' = 3x^2 - 2x$ ✓, $(2\sqrt{x})' = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$ ✓.

**Watch out:** Raising the power $-\frac{1}{2}$ by one gives $+\frac{1}{2}$, not $-\frac{3}{2}$; dividing by the new power brings the factor $2$.

**Answer:** $18$ and $2$.
