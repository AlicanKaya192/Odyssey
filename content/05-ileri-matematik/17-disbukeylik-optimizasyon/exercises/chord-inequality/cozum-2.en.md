**Idea:** Write the gap between the chord and the function with algebra, so that no numbers are needed to see it cannot be negative.

**Step 1 — The gap.**

$$
\frac{1 + e^2}{2} - e = \frac{1 - 2e + e^2}{2} = \frac{(e - 1)^2}{2}
$$

**Step 2 — The sign.** Half a square: always $\ge 0$. The chord is above the function.

**Step 3 — The numbers.** $f(1) = e \approx 2.718$; the gap is $\frac{1.71828^2}{2} \approx 1.476$; the chord $\approx 4.195$.

**Why the same result?** The same comparison, now in general form: for the midpoint, $\frac{e^a + e^b}{2} - e^{(a + b)/2} = \frac{1}{2}\left(e^{a/2} - e^{b/2}\right)^2 \ge 0$ holds for every $a$, $b$. The first solution is a single numerical example; this one shows why that example always works.

**Answer:** $2.718$ and $4.195$.
