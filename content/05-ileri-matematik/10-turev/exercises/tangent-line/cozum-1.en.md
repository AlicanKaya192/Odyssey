**What is asked?** The tangent line to a curve at a point.

**Idea:** The slope of the tangent is the derivative at that point; the tangent passes through that point. The slope and the point give the point–slope form.

**Step 1 — The derivative.** $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 2$, $m = f'(1) = 1$.

**Step 2 — The point.** $f(1) = 1 - 2 = -1$: the point $(1, -1)$.

**Step 3 — The tangent.** $y + 1 = 1 \cdot (x - 1)$, that is, $y = x - 2$: $b = -2$.

**Check:** At $x = 1.1$ the curve is $1.331 - 2.2 = -0.869$ and the tangent $-0.9$. Close; the tangent follows the curve well around the point ✓.

**Watch out:** Writing the tangent as $y = f'(x)(x - 1) + f(1)$, with a slope that depends on $x$, is wrong; the slope is a number, $f'(1)$.

**Answer:** $m = 1$, $b = -2$.
