**What is asked?** The gradient at a point and the rate of change in a given direction.

**Idea:** The directional derivative is the dot product of the gradient with the unit direction vector.

**Step 1 — The gradient.** $\nabla f = (2x, 6y)$; at $(2, 1)$ it is $(4, 6)$.

**Step 2 — Is the direction a unit vector?** $\lVert \mathbf{u} \rVert^2 = \frac{9 + 16}{25} = 1$ ✓.

**Step 3 — Multiply.** $(4, 6) \cdot \left(\frac{3}{5}, \frac{4}{5}\right) = \frac{12 + 24}{5} = 7.2$.

**Check:** The fastest increase is $\lVert (4, 6) \rVert = \sqrt{52} \approx 7.211$. $7.2$ is smaller but very close: $\mathbf{u}$ points almost the same way as the gradient ✓.

**Watch out:** Using the non-unit $(3, 4)$ would give $36$, five times too much.

**Answer:** $4$, $6$ and $7.2$.
