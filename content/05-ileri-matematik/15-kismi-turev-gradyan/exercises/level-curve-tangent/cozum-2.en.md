**Idea:** The curve's equation is $x^2 + y^2 = 25$. Treat $y$ as a function of $x$ and differentiate both sides with respect to $x$.

**Step 1 — Differentiate.** $2x + 2y \, y' = 0$, so $y' = -\frac{x}{y}$.

**Step 2 — At the point.** $y' = -\frac{3}{4}$.

**Step 3 — The line.** $b = 4 - \left(-\frac{3}{4}\right) \cdot 3 = 4 + \frac{9}{4} = \frac{25}{4}$.

**Why the same result?** The general rule $y' = -\frac{f_x}{f_y}$: since $f$ does not change along the curve, $f_x + f_y y' = 0$. That says the dot product of the direction $(1, y')$ with the gradient is zero, which is perpendicularity itself.

**Answer:** $-\frac{3}{4}$ and $\frac{25}{4}$.
