**Idea:** Complete the square separately in the $x$ terms and the $y$ terms; since squares are never negative, the smallest value can be read off at once.

**Step 1 — Complete the squares.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= (x^2 - 4x) + (y^2 + 6y) + 5 \\
&= (x - 2)^2 - 4 + (y + 3)^2 - 9 + 5 \\
&= (x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 - 8
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2 — Read it off.** Both squares are at least $0$, and both are zero at $x = 2$, $y = -3$. The smallest value is $-8$.

**Why the same result?** The level curves are circles centred at $(2, -3)$; the gradient points outwards from the centre everywhere and is zero only at the centre. Setting the partial derivatives to zero is the derivative's way of setting the squares to zero.

**Answer:** $2$, $-3$ and $-8$.
