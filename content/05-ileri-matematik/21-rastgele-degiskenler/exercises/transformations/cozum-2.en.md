**Idea:** The six values of $Y$ are equally likely; apply the definition directly.

**Step 1 — The values.** $Y = 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11$. Mean $\frac{36}{6} = 6$.

**Step 2 — The variance.** The deviations are $-5, -3, -1, 1, 3, 5$; their squares $25, 9, 1, 1, 9, 25$, total $70$. $\frac{70}{6} = \frac{35}{3}$.

**Step 3 — Three dice.** One die's variance from its deviations: $\frac{2 \cdot (6.25 + 2.25 + 0.25)}{6} = \frac{17.5}{6} = \frac{35}{12}$; three independent dice give $\frac{35}{4}$.

**Why the same result?** The deviations of $Y$ are exactly twice those of $X$ ($\pm 2.5 \to \pm 5$) and their squares four times; the $a^2$ rule is this observation in general.

**Answer:** $6$, $\frac{35}{3}$ and $\frac{35}{4}$.
