**What is asked?** The expectation and variance of a linear transformation, and the variance of an independent sum.

**Idea:** Expectation is linear; variance squares the factor and ignores the shift; under independence variances add.

**Step 1 — $E[Y]$.** $2 \cdot 3.5 - 1 = 6$.

**Step 2 — $\operatorname{Var}(Y)$.** $2^2 \cdot \frac{35}{12} = \frac{140}{12} = \frac{35}{3} \approx 11.67$.

**Step 3 — Three dice.** $3 \cdot \frac{35}{12} = \frac{35}{4} = 8.75$.

**Check:** The values of $Y$ are $1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11$; their mean is $6$ ✓.

**Watch out:** $\operatorname{Var}(3X)$ and "the sum of three dice" differ: $\operatorname{Var}(3X) = 9 \cdot \frac{35}{12} = 26.25$, because one die is tripled; three separate dice partly balance each other's deviations.

**Answer:** $6$, $\frac{35}{3}$, $\frac{35}{4}$.
