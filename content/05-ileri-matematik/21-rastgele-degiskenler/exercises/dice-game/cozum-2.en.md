**Idea:** Think of $36$ games in which each outcome comes up exactly once; divide the total gain by $36$.

**Step 1 — Sum $7$.** $6$ times in $36$ games: probability $\frac{6}{36}$.

**Step 2 — Total gain.** $30$ six times ($180$), $60$ twice ($120$), $-8$ twenty-eight times ($-224$): total $76$. Per game $\frac{76}{36} \approx 2.11$.

**Step 3 — The fair loss.** The wins total $300$; the $28$ losses must cancel it: $28L = 300$, $L \approx 10.71$.

**Why the same result?** The expected value is "the average per game over many games"; $36$ games in which each outcome appears in exact proportion is a perfect instance of that average.

**Answer:** $\frac{1}{6}$, $\frac{19}{9}$ and $\frac{75}{7}$.
